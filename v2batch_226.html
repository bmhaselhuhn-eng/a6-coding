What counts as a western blot. Western blot means proteins separated on a gel, transferred to a membrane and detected with an antibody. An OxyBlot counts. A dot blot or slot blot, of RNA or of protein, does not; neither does a capillary system such as Wes or Jess, which has no membrane, or a membrane probed only with streptavidin or a lectin, which uses no antibody. Items marked 'Western blot only' do not count the same thing described for another assay in the paper: qPCR, immunofluorescence, IHC, flow cytometry, ELISA, mass spectrometry, or a gel that was stained rather than blotted. Judge the text the page shows, including any figure legend printed in it; a detail found only in a legend or supplement that is not shown is not stated. A method given only by citation ('as previously described [12]') is not stated: mark only what the text itself says. The Shortcut citation item is the exception: it records the citation phrase itself. Some PDFs drop the micro sign or print it as m ('50 g', '25 mg' for micrograms); rule on the intended unit.

Far-western blotting is an effective technique to assess protein-protein interactions, including receptor-ligand interactions (Kaido et al., 2007; Wu et al., 2007), in in vitro assays. In a far-western blotting analysis, one protein is first separated in an SDS-PAGE gel and transferred to a membrane, followed by the binding of a non-antibody secondary protein. Then, a specific antibody against the secondary protein will be employed to determine its binding to the first protein that is being transferred onto the membrane (Figure 1). Each x-, y-datum pair in a data set corresponded to the antibody bind- ing intensity (y) at a specific distance (x) from the origin of protein migration. Protein MW markers provided a basis to normalize x-axis distances across blots such that the x-axes of mul- tiple blots could be equivalently scaled. The equation p = a ●ek ●x was used to describe p, pre- dicted log (protein size), as a function of distance migrated, x, on the Western blot where ‘a’ and ‘k’ were constants that dictated the shape of the standard curve. The method of least squares and the “Solver” tool in Microsoft Excel was used to optimize the values of k and a, such that the difference between p, predicted log (protein size), and known log (protein size) of MW markers was minimized between 35 and 130 kDa.                       Typing is what this design removes. Given a sentence like the one below, one person types RIPA, another types the whole parenthesis, a third types the clause. All three read the paper correctly and disagree in the data. Selecting removes that entirely.

                      How much to select

                      The shortest phrase that states the detail. Not the whole sentence when three words will do, and not the vendor, city or catalog number unless the question asks for them.

                      Every question shows a worked example under What to mark. Follow it rather than your instinct.

                      When a paper gives several

                      Many papers list a table of antibodies with eight RRIDs, eight catalog numbers and several vendors. Mark them all. Press Use highlighted text once per value and they stack up in a numbered list. Press Done, next when the list is complete.

                      If they sit in a contiguous table or a run-on list, selecting the whole block in one go is fine and much faster. You are marking where the evidence is, not counting.

                      The same value repeated does not need marking twice. A paper that uses 1:1000 for six antibodies has one dilution, not six.

                      If you stop before you have them all, tick 'There are more of these than I marked'. That box matters more than it looks: without it, someone who stopped at five of eight cannot be told apart from someone who found everything, and the three they skipped would count against the extractor as mistakes it did not make.

                      What counts

                      Judge only what is written. Not what the authors almost certainly did, and not what is standard practice in the field.

                      Judge the text shown. The page shows the Methods section, and sometimes figure legends or the full text where no Methods heading was found. A detail in the text shown counts, legend included. A detail found only in a legend or supplementary file that is not shown is Not stated.

                      Unclear is a real answer. It is set aside rather than counted either way, so use it when you are genuinely stuck rather than guessing.

                      The shaded sentences

                      The faint tint marks the western blot section: sentences that name the blot, the gel, the membrane or its detection. It is the same for every question on a paper. Blot section jumps between its blocks. Other assays are left untinted, but an item can still be stated elsewhere, for example in a figure legend.

                      The warmer marks are the sentences most likely to hold the answer to the open question, and Prev and Next step between them.

                      Both are search aids and nothing more. Fixed keyword rules pick them; they are never told the answer, they sometimes shade irrelevant sentences and sometimes miss the right one. Read around them, and judge only the text.

                      Every question, in one place

                      Reducing or denaturing conditionsA statement of reducing or denaturing conditions for the blot's samples: Laemmli buffer; sample or loading buffer with heating; a stated denaturing temperature; a reducing agent (DTT, 2-ME); the condition named ('denatured'); or an explicit non-reducing statement. A reducing agent in a lysis or fractionation buffer feeding the blot counts. SDS-PAGE alone, an SDS running buffer, or heating in elution buffer does not. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the phrase or the reagent that states it. In boiled in Laemmli buffer containing DTT mark DTT, or boiled in Laemmli buffer if no reagent is named. Nothing to mark in separated by SDS-PAGE, SDS running buffer, or heated in elution buffer.
                      Sample buffer compositionThe loading or sample buffer's recipe, or the product used. A recipe with at least one component and its concentration counts, and so does a product identified by brand name, vendor or catalog number. 'Laemmli buffer' or 'loading buffer' alone does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the recipe, or the product with what identifies it. In 4x NuPAGE LDS Sample Buffer mark NuPAGE LDS Sample Buffer.
                      Electrophoresis conditionsThe voltage or current, or the run time, for the blot's gel. Either counts. Transfer voltage or time does not. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the conditions. In run at 120 V for 90 min mark the whole phrase.
                      Blot protocol depositedA statement that a detailed protocol for the blot is deposited in a protocol repository or protocol journal (protocols.io, Protocol Exchange, Bio-protocol), with its link or DOI. A citation to a research paper, a manufacturer's protocol, or a lab website does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the statement with its link or DOI. In the full protocol is available at protocols.io (dx.doi.org/10.17504/...) mark the whole phrase.
                      Quantification procedureHow the blot's bands were quantified beyond naming software: background subtraction, the measurement (integrated density, area under the peak), or how bands were selected. 'Densitometry was performed' does not count, and neither does normalization alone, which is its own item. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the words that describe the procedure. In integrated density was measured after rolling-ball background subtraction mark the whole phrase.
                      Shortcut citationA statement that the blot method, or a step of it, was done as described in a cited source ('as previously described [12]', 'according to Smith et al.'), with or without details given. A citation for a reagent, for an antibody's validation, or for another assay does not count, and neither does following a manufacturer's instructions. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the phrase that points to the citation. In Western blotting was performed as previously described (24) mark as previously described (24). Nothing to mark in qPCR was performed as previously described (12).
                      Stripping verifiedA statement that stripping was checked as complete before reprobing, for example by re-imaging the stripped membrane with substrate or incubating it with secondary antibody alone. That the membrane was stripped, or the stripping method, does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the statement. In removal of antibody was confirmed by re-exposing the stripped membrane to ECL mark the whole phrase. Nothing to mark in membranes were stripped and reprobed.
                      Wash conditionsThe wash buffer, the number of washes, or their duration for the blot. Any one counts; 'washed' alone does not. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the words that state the washes. In washed three times for 10 min in TBST mark the whole phrase.
                      Reducing agent, namedA named reducing agent in the sample or loading buffer for any sample on the blot's gel (a reduced control lane counts), or in a lysis or fractionation buffer whose lysate is run on the blot: DTT, beta-mercaptoethanol (2-ME), TCEP, or a named reducing product such as NuPAGE Sample Reducing Agent. An agent named only as left out ('without DTT') does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark each distinct reagent name once. In Laemmli buffer with 5% 2-mercaptoethanol mark 2-mercaptoethanol. Nothing to mark in boiled in Laemmli buffer, with no reagent named, or in DTT used for a mass-spec digest.

                      Ask about a rule

                      This answers questions about what a rule means, nothing else.
