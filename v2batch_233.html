What counts as a western blot. Western blot means proteins separated on a gel, transferred to a membrane and detected with an antibody. An OxyBlot counts. A dot blot or slot blot, of RNA or of protein, does not; neither does a capillary system such as Wes or Jess, which has no membrane, or a membrane probed only with streptavidin or a lectin, which uses no antibody. Items marked 'Western blot only' do not count the same thing described for another assay in the paper: qPCR, immunofluorescence, IHC, flow cytometry, ELISA, mass spectrometry, or a gel that was stained rather than blotted. Judge the text the page shows, including any figure legend printed in it; a detail found only in a legend or supplement that is not shown is not stated. A method given only by citation ('as previously described [12]') is not stated: mark only what the text itself says. The Shortcut citation item is the exception: it records the citation phrase itself. Some PDFs drop the micro sign or print it as m ('50 g', '25 mg' for micrograms); rule on the intended unit.

Every question carries its own What to mark rule with a worked example. Follow it rather than your instinct: consistency between reviewers matters more here than any single choice. Full rules under Guide.

Shortcut citation
A statement that the blot method, or a step of it, was done as described in a cited source ('as previously described [12]', 'according to Smith et al.'), with or without details given. A citation for a reagent, for an antibody's validation, or for another assay does not count, and neither does following a manufacturer's instructions. Western blot only.

        2.1 Vector construction and bacterial strains The vector pNMD46772 containing the ColM-coding cma gene under the control of a 35S promoter and selective bar gene under the control of a nos promoter was transformed to Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain GV3101. The transformed bacteria were grown on selective LB (Bertani, 1951) agar medium containing rifampicin 50 mg/L, gentamicin 25 mg/L and kanamycin 100 mg/L. Colonies were confirmed to be transformed with vector pNMD46772 using PCR analysis with primers specific for the cma gene. 2.2 Plant transformation and regeneration Agrobacterium-mediated transformation of lettuce ‘Odeskyj curling’; with the vector pNMD46772 containing the cma gene was carried out as described in Shcherbak et al. (2013). After 48 h of co-cultivation with diluted Agrobacterium suspension supplemented with 100 mM acetosyringone, explants were placed on modified B5 (Gamborg et al., 1968) regeneration medium (B5 medium salts with 2.5% sucrose, 3 mg/L kinetin, 0.5 mg/L NAA) supplemented with 5 mg/L phosfinothricin (PPT) for selection. As shoot appeared, each one was removed and placed on the MS (Murashige and Skoog, 1962) medium supplemented with 0.5 mg/L of indole-3-butyric acid for rooting. Well established plants were transferred into the soil and grown in a greenhouse. For genetic transformation of mizuna, hypocotyls of 5-7-day- old seedlings were used as explants. After co-cultivation with Agrobacterium, the explants were cultivated on callus induction MS medium containing 2,4-D (2 mg/L) and BAP (0.5 mg/L) for one week. Further regeneration of shoots took place on a nutrient medium containing zeatin (2 mg/L), BAP (1 mg/L) and NAA (0.5 mg/L). PPT at concentrations of 5 mg/L was used for the first step of mizuna transformants selection and after two weeks of cultivation on the regeneration medium the PPT concentration was increased to 10 mg/L. Regenerated plants were also rooted, transferred to the soil and grown in a greenhouse. 2.3 PCR and RT-PCR Genomic DNA was isolated from plant leaves using the CTAB protocol for isolating DNA from plant tissues. PCR was carried out in 20 ml reaction volume containing 50 ng DNA, 200 mM each of forward and reverse primers, 200 mM dNTPs and 1 U Taq DNA polymerase (Thermo Scientific, USA). Transient expression in Nicotiana benthamiana has shown great potential for the manufacturing of recombinant biopharmaceuticals. Consequently, a wide range of medicinal proteins including recombinant vaccine antigens, monoclonal antibodies and other biotherapeutics have been synthesized in plants, some of which have advanced to clinical development (Daniell et al., 2009; Bendandi et al., 2010; Chichester et al., 2012; Gleba et al., 2014; Tusé et al., 2015; Ward et al., 2020; Leroux-Roels et al., 2022). With regard to antimicrobial proteins specifically, the cost of goods sold (COGS) for such proteins made by transient expression in plants at commercial scale was estimated to be comparable to those of products made by traditional food processing methods (McNulty et al., 2020). Despite the undoubted effectiveness of transient expression systems, the stable expression of antimicrobial proteins in transgenic edible plants makes it possible to reduce the costs of the most expensive part of production, namely, the extraction and purification of the recombinant protein (Łojewska et al., 2016). Such vegetables can be used as an antibacterial food or feed supplement without, or with minimal, downstream purification of the product. Mizuna is a traditional and very popular crop in Asia and is increasingly attracting the attention of European and US consumers. Therefore, we developed mizuna plants expressing the ColM gene to impart antibacterial protective functions on a crop that is becoming well-accepted by consumers. In addition, and on a fundamental level, mizuna, in contrast to lettuce, has not been studied as a target for genetic transformation. To our knowledge, we are the first to report not only the successful genetic transformation of mizuna with the cma gene, but also the first genetic transformation of this plant species. According to published data, the transient expression of ColM has been described in the standard host Nicotiana benthamiana, as well as in edible plant hosts such as spinach and leafy beets. Purified ColM obtained in plants by the transient expression method had the broadest antimicrobial activity against pathogenic strains of E. coli and, in mixtures, supplemented the effectiveness of other colicins (Schulz et al., 2015; Stephan et al., 2017). In addition, transgenic N. benthamiana (Hahn-Löbmann et al., 2019) and N. tabacum L. plants expressing ColM were developed (Łojewska et al., 2020) and the antibacterial activity of purified ColM obtained from these plants was confirmed against an array of pathogenic strains, including clinical isolates of E. coli and Klebsiella pneumonia. In the present study, we describe for the first time the antibacterial activity of edible transgenic plants stably expressing recombinant ColM. Our data demonstrate that ColM can be stably expressed in edible plants at a level that provides the desired antibacterial functionality. We also confirmed both the antibacterial activity of transgenic plant extracts and the inheritance of this trait in subsequent generations. Our studies once again confirm that ColM expressed in plants retains full functional activity against E. coli pathogenic serotype O104:H4 and O157:H7. Furthermore, extracts of edible plants containing ColM were found to inhibit the growth of multidrug-resistant strains of E. coli that produce beta-lactamases and carbapenemases. These results are of practical importance as carbapenems are considered to be antibiotics of last resort and antimicrobial options for the treatment of carbapenem- resistant Enterobacteriaceae (CRE) are limited (Sheu et al., 2019).                       Guide

                      You do not type anything

                      Select the words in the Methods text, then press Use highlighted text. The exact words you selected are what gets recorded.

                      Typing is what this design removes. Given a sentence like the one below, one person types RIPA, another types the whole parenthesis, a third types the clause. All three read the paper correctly and disagree in the data. Selecting removes that entirely.

                      The cell total protein was isolated using RIPA (Applygen Technologies Inc., Beijing, China). Protease inhibitor (CWBIO, Shanghai, China) was added into the RIPA at a ratio of 1:100.

                      For lysis buffer there, mark just RIPA, the first time it appears.

                      How much to select

                      The shortest phrase that states the detail. Not the whole sentence when three words will do, and not the vendor, city or catalog number unless the question asks for them.

                      Every question shows a worked example under What to mark. Follow it rather than your instinct.

                      When a paper gives several

                      Many papers list a table of antibodies with eight RRIDs, eight catalog numbers and several vendors. Mark them all. Press Use highlighted text once per value and they stack up in a numbered list. Press Done, next when the list is complete.

                      If they sit in a contiguous table or a run-on list, selecting the whole block in one go is fine and much faster. You are marking where the evidence is, not counting.

                      The same value repeated does not need marking twice. A paper that uses 1:1000 for six antibodies has one dilution, not six.

                      If you stop before you have them all, tick 'There are more of these than I marked'. That box matters more than it looks: without it, someone who stopped at five of eight cannot be told apart from someone who found everything, and the three they skipped would count against the extractor as mistakes it did not make.

                      What counts

                      Judge only what is written. Not what the authors almost certainly did, and not what is standard practice in the field.

                      Judge the text shown. The page shows the Methods section, and sometimes figure legends or the full text where no Methods heading was found. A detail in the text shown counts, legend included. A detail found only in a legend or supplementary file that is not shown is Not stated.

                      Unclear is a real answer. It is set aside rather than counted either way, so use it when you are genuinely stuck rather than guessing.

                      The shaded sentences

                      The faint tint marks the western blot section: sentences that name the blot, the gel, the membrane or its detection. It is the same for every question on a paper. Blot section jumps between its blocks. Other assays are left untinted, but an item can still be stated elsewhere, for example in a figure legend.

                      The warmer marks are the sentences most likely to hold the answer to the open question, and Prev and Next step between them.

                      Both are search aids and nothing more. Fixed keyword rules pick them; they are never told the answer, they sometimes shade irrelevant sentences and sometimes miss the right one. Read around them, and judge only the text.

                      Getting through a batch

                      The Methods text stays pinned on the left while questions scroll on the right. Answers collapse to one line and the next question opens automatically. Click any collapsed line to change it.

                      Your work is saved in this browser as you go, so closing the tab loses nothing. Submit is at the top of the page and turns on once every question is answered and your initials are filled in.
