What counts as a western blot. Western blot means proteins separated on a gel, transferred to a membrane and detected with an antibody. An OxyBlot counts. A dot blot or slot blot, of RNA or of protein, does not; neither does a capillary system such as Wes or Jess, which has no membrane, or a membrane probed only with streptavidin or a lectin, which uses no antibody. Items marked 'Western blot only' do not count the same thing described for another assay in the paper: qPCR, immunofluorescence, IHC, flow cytometry, ELISA, mass spectrometry, or a gel that was stained rather than blotted. Judge the text the page shows, including any figure legend printed in it; a detail found only in a legend or supplement that is not shown is not stated. A method given only by citation ('as previously described [12]') is not stated: mark only what the text itself says. The Shortcut citation item is the exception: it records the citation phrase itself. Some PDFs drop the micro sign or print it as m ('50 g', '25 mg' for micrograms); rule on the intended unit.

Every question carries its own What to mark rule with a worked example. Follow it rather than your instinct: consistency between reviewers matters more here than any single choice. Full rules under Guide.

      Study population Details of the design, methods, and study participants in the China Kadoorie Biobank study have been previously described.15 Briefly, the baseline survey was done between June, 2004, and July, 2008, in ten (five urban and five rural) geo­graphically diverse areas across China (Qingdao, Haikou, Harbin, Suzhou, Liuzhou, Henan, Sichuan, Hunan, Gansu, and Zhejiang) and enrolled 512 715 adults aged 30-79 years. The ten regions were selected with the aim to maximise the diversity from the geographical locations, socioeconomic characteristics, risk exposures, and disease patterns, while considering the quality of death and disease registries and local capacity. In each study area, a regional coordinating centre and survey team were set up and all permanent residents aged 30-79 years identified from local residential records were invited to participate. Extensive data were collected through a laptop-based questionnaire (eg, socio­demo­graphic, lifestyle and dietary factors, and medical history) and physical measurements (eg, blood pressure and adiposity), along with the collection of a 10 mL blood sample for long-term storage. Ethics approval was obtained from relevant international, national, and local ethics committees (in the UK, Oxford Tropical Research Ethics Committee; in China, the Chinese Centre for Disease Control and Prevention Ethical Review Committee and the Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences and Peking Union Medical College Ethical Committee). All participants provided written informed consent. Follow-up of participants in the China Kadoorie Biobank study was done through linkage, via unique personal identification numbers, to established mor­tality and morbidity registries (for cancer, stroke, ischaemic heart disease, and diabetes) that were already available in the study areas since the beginning of the baseline survey, and to the nationwide health insurance system, which records any episodes of patients being admitted to and staying in hospital as inpatients. By Jan 1, 2017, 44 037 (8·6%) participants had died, 4781 (<1%) were lost to follow-up, and 27 903 (5·4%) developed cancer, including 3464 gastric cancers (International Classification of Disease 10 [ICD-10]: C16). For any reported cancer cases, systematic validation was done through a review of the original medical records (including histopathological reports) retrieved from hospitals. Among the 1355 rep­ orted gastric cancer cases that were adjudicated, 1246 (92%) were confirmed, and among the confirmed cases with pathology results available, 1060 (85%) of 1246 were adenocarcinomas. Design of case-cohort study To reduce the potential effects of lower H pylori antibody concentrations due to gastric atrophy, the selection of incident gastric cancer cases were confined to participants who were alive and with no history of cancer 2 years after study entry. All 437 reported or confirmed CGC cases (ICD-10: C16·0) were selected, and we randomly selected 500 cases from the 762 confirmed NCGC (of 2035 reported) cases. Since CGC arises in the oesophageal-gastric junction and is often difficult to distinguish from oesophageal adenocarcinoma, which is rare in China, we also included all confirmed oe­ sophageal adenocarcinoma cases (n=27). A subcohort of 500 participants was sampled from the modified baseline cohort (ie, participants who were alive with no history of cancer 2 years after study entry) using stratified random sampling with the sizes of the strata based on the proportions of age categories and sex of all selected participants with gastric cancer (appendix p 2). This modified baseline subcohort included approximately 100 000 previously genotyped participants who were randomly selected from the whole cohort. Immunoblot assay A qualitative western blot kit assay, HelicoBlot 2.1 (MP Diagnostics; Bordeaux, France), was used to detect IgG antibodies to H pylori proteins in the baseline plasma samples retrieved from storage in liquid nitrogen. The assay detects seven diagnostic antibodies binding to H pylori proteins separated on a nitrocellulose membrane and has been shown to have a sensitivity of 96% and a specificity of 93% for the detection of H pylori infections in populations in Europe.16 The immunoblot assay results were independently classified and reviewed by two laboratory staff (one of which was DC) who were masked to the baseline information and case status of the samples. Each H pylori biomarker band was classified as positive, negative, or ambiguous. The clinical criteria, as defined by the manufacturer of the western blot kit, were the presence of one or more of the 89kD (vacuolating cytotoxin), 37kD, or 35kD bands; or both the 30kD (urease enzyme light subunit) and 19·5kD bands; or both the 116kD (cytotoxin-associated antigen) and the current infection marker bands. For this study, we modified the criteria by removing the requirement to have the current infection marker band alongside the cytotoxin-associated antigen band (ie, the cytotoxin-associated antigen band alone can define seropositivity). The TGF-β signaling pathway is critical in tumorigenesis, influencing processes such as carcinoma cell invasion, metastasis, chemoresistance, and immune evasion [17-19]. In CRC, the CMS4 subgroup, character­ ized by mesenchymal features and significant TGF-β activation, is associated with relatively poor outcomes [20]. While it is well-documented that TGF-β contributes to chemoresistance through mechanisms such as epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and other molecular pathways [19,21], research has shown that its activa­ tion triggers SMAD complex activation, which regulates target gene expression by recruiting epigenetic regulators like histone and DNA modifiers [22]. Moreover, cell-type-specific DNA and histone modifi­ cation patterns add another regulatory layer by influencing gene accessibility via TGF-β/SMAD3 [23]. Previous studie shows that TGF-β activation disrupts the GATA4-JMJD3 interaction, blocking H3K27 demethylation and impairing gene expression in cardiac reprogram­ ming; however, similar findings in CRC are scarce [24]. In our investigation, we conducted a genome-scale examination of histone modification profiles associated with the TGF-β signaling pathway, aiming to elucidate its role in the regulation of oxaliplatin chemoresistance in CRC. This endeavor seeks to uncover potential mechanisms linking drug resistance to the TGF-β signaling pathway and offers a foundation for identifying new therapeutic targets to enhance chemoresistance in CRC. Material & methods Clinical specimens Paraffin-fixed colorectal cancer tissues were randomly obtained from 154 CRC patients between 2017 and 2022 at Shanghai Renji Hospital. Written informed consents were obtained from all participants in this study. Our follow-up of these patients began in 2012 with a minimum follow-up time of no less than half a year. All the patients were classified into two groups based on tumor recurrence; the patients were termed as CRC recurrent group if relapsed during the time of following up, and the other patients belonged to CRC nonrecurrent group. Our study was implemented by following the provisions of the Declaration of Helsinki of 1975. Ethical consent was granted from the ethics committee of Renji Hospital,Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine. Cell lines Human colon cancer cell lines SW480 (RRID:CVCL_0546) and HCT116 (RRID:CVCL_0291) were purchased from ATCC, and have been authenticated using short tandem repeat profiling by Beijing Microread Genetics Co., Ltd. All experiments were performed with mycoplasma- free cells. Animal study All animal procedures were conducted in accordance with the Na­ tional Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (NIH Publications No. 8023, revised 1978) and received approval from the Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine Animal Care and Use Committee. In our research, male BALB/c nude mice (4-5 weeks old) were used (Experimental Animal Centre, SIBS). The colorectal cancer xenograft models were built through injecting SW480 cells (3 × 106) subcutane­ ously into the right axilla of these mice. The mice were randomly divided into different groups 5 days later after subcutaneous inoculation. To explore the role of HDAC4 in TGF-β1 associated chemoresistance in vivo, 4 groups were set in the study xenograft models; each group consisted of an experimental subgroup and a control subgroup. Groups were as follow: i) empty viral vector group, ii) HDAC4 overexpression adenovirus group, iii) TGF-β1 intervention group, iv) TGF-β1 and HDAC4 overexpression adenovirus group. All 4 experimental subgroups were treated with oxaliplatin, while 4 corresponding control subgroups were not; they were set as to be the baseline to eliminate the effect of TGF-β1 on tumor proliferation, as shown in Table S1. The tumor growth inhibitory rate was calculated by the formula: (Ve- Vc)/Ve × 100 %, where “Ve” and “Vc” were the tumor volume in experiment subgroup and control subgroup, respectively. Tumor volume was calculated by the formula: a × b2/2, where “a” and “b” represented the width and length of the tumors, respectively. The volumes of tumors were measured twice per week with calipers (in millimeters), and the tumor volumes data were presented as means±SD. TGF-β1 was given by multipoint intratumoral injection, 100 ng each time, once a week for three weeks. The relevant adenovirus vectors were given by multipoint intratumoral injection, twice per week for three weeks. Chemotherapeutic agent oxaliplatin (7.5mg/kg) was adminis­ tered by intraperitoneal injection, twice a week for three weeks. The concentrations of TGF-β1 and oxaliplatin used in our experi­ ments were selected based on previous studies. TGF-β1 was adminis­ tered at 10 ng/mL every other day in vitro, as demonstrated by Sun et al., who showed that this dosage effectively induces epithelial-to- mesenchymal transition [25]. Similarly, the concentration of oxalipla­ tin in our experiments was informed by earlier research,which found that these levels effectively inhibit tumor cell proliferation both in vivo and in vitro. In our study, we further optimized the oxaliplatin dose to balance its efficacy with minimized toxicity [26,27]. The conventional experimental techniques in our study, including quantitative real-time PCR Western blot analysis, Immunohistochemical Staining (IHC), RNA- seq and CHIP-seq, etc. were detailed in Table S2. The CHIP-seq data and RNA-seq data in our study are accessible through GEO Series accession number GSE141702 and GSE137779, respectively. Statistical analysis All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 3.4.2) (http://cran.r-project.org). DAVID analysis was used to generate the associated biological pathways (https://david.ncifcrf.gov/). For correlation analysis, R package (PerformanceAnalytics) was introduced to calculate the correlation coefficient and P-value, and the value of IHC score was taken the square root to meet the requirement of normal distribution. As to the analysis of clinicopathologic features, the Pear­ son’s Chi-squared test was performed. The recurrence-free survival was estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method, and the log-rank test was used to evaluate the differences between survival curves. A t-test was employed to compare differences between the two groups under various treatments. This includes evaluating the effect of TGF-β intervention on HDAC4 expression, assessing the impact of HDAC4 knockdown or overexpression on oxaliplatin-induced inhibition of colorectal cancer cell proliferation and apoptosis, and comparing tumor growth suppres­ sion in xenografts across different interventions. P-values <0.05 were considered to be of significant difference, and all P-values were two- sided unless otherwise specified. It is well known that TGF-β1 intervention can induce EMT transition, accompanying with the changes of cell adhesion [31]. As shown in Supplementary Fig 3, the target genes of H3K18ac were closely involved in the function of cell molecular adhesion binding, and growth factor binding, etc. Conversely, few bio­ logical processes were identified through analyzing the target genes of H3K27ac, suggesting that this epigenetic mark might play a minor role in the activation of TGF-β1 signaling pathway. We have demonstrated the significant differences in the levels of H3K9ac and H3K18ac asso­ ciated with TGF-β1 signaling pathway, and the functional enrichment analysis of target genes of H3K18ac partially reflected the activation of this signaling pathway, indicating the reliability of our CHIP-seq data. Our experimental data revealed that the activation of TGF-β1 signaling pathway was characterized by a broad epigenetic change-relative high expression of H3K9ac and H3K18ac. These findings indicated that the acetylation modification of H3K9 and H3K18 probably underlay the biological functions associated with TGF-β1 pathway, the detailed mechanism of which warrants further investigation. The acetylation status of the genome is orchestrated by the expres­ sion of histone acetyltransferases or histone deacetylases. The global up- regulation of histone acetylation modification after the activation of the TGF-β signaling pathway raised the possibility that the differential expression of specific histone acetyltransferases or histone deacetylases may involve in the modulation of TGF-β-associated chemoresistance in CRC. To test this hypothesis, high-throughput mRNA sequencing (RNA- Seq) was performed to compare the expression profile between SW480 cells treated with or without TGF-β1. TGF-β1 treatment downregulated 2891 gene expressions and upregulated 2998 gene expressions in SW480 cells (adjusted p-value <0.05, raw data accessible via GEO: GSE137779). RNA-seq analysis revealed that, among the current known histone acetyltransferases, only Gcn5 was upregulated (1/15), but the difference was not statistically significant. Further analysis of deacety­ lases indicated that HDAC4, HDAC9, and SIRT7 were downregulated (3/ 21), with statistically significant differences, as shown in Table S4. Western blot and real-time PCR (RT-PCR) showed that HDAC4 was significantly downregulated after TGF-β1 intervention (Fig. 1D). More­ over, the expression of HDAC4 was closely related to the modulation of histone acetylation modification. Down-regulation of HDAC4 was demonstrated to upregulate the acetylation levels of H3K9 and H3K18 (Fig. 1E), while ectopic overexpression of HDAC4 showed the opposite results (Fig. 1F). However, no significant upregulation of H3K9ac and H3K18ac was found after knockdown of HDAC9 or SIRT7 (Supple­ mentary Fig 4). These findings indicated that the activation of the TGF-β signaling pathway led to the hyperacetylation of H3K9 and H3K18 via modulating the expression of HDAC4, which may play a momentous role in TGF-β-associated chemoresistance. In CCK-8 assay, after knockdown the expression of HDAC4 using siRNA specific for HDAC4, the inhibition rate of oxaliplatin on cell proliferation lowered significantly in SW480 cells (Fig. 2A) and HCT116 cells (Fig. 2B) compared to the normal control (NC). To further confirm the above experimental results, we applied flow cytometry to detect cell apoptosis in colorectal cancer cells. HDAC4 knockdown can decrease the oxaliplatin-induced cell apoptosis signifi­ cantly in SW480 (Fig. 2C&E) and HCT116 cells (Fig. 2D&F). The expression of apoptotic proteins was investigated by western blot. When oxaliplatin was not administrated, knockdown of HDAC4 had no sig­ nificant effect on cell apoptosis in SW480 cells. Over­ expression of HDAC4 significantly enhanced the chemosensitivity to oxaliplatin. To confirm whether the TGF- β-associated chemoresistance is regulated by HDAC4, we conducted to investigate the effect of overexpression of HDAC4 on chemoresistance in the context of activation of the TGF-β signaling pathway. Studies have demonstrated the TGF-β mediated drug resistance in tumors [21]. Our data showed consistent results through CCK-8 assay in colorectal cancer cells. Different concentrations of oxaliplatin were administrated to TGF-β1-treated SW480 and HCT116 cells. As shown in Fig. 4A&C, the growth inhibition rate of cells in TGF-β1 group was significantly lower than the NC group. Conversely, overexpression of HDAC4 in TGF-β1-treated colorectal cancer cells could enhance the chemosensitivity to oxaliplatin Fig. 4B&D, and the differences were statistically significant. Flow cytometry was also applied to detect cell apoptosis induced by oxaliplatin. Cell apoptosis decreased significantly in the TGF-β1-treated SW480 and HCT116 cells, however, over­ expression of HDAC4 abolished the TGF-β1-induced drug resistance, thus the apoptosis proportion increased significantly when over­ expressing of HDAC4 in SW480 (Fig. 4E) and HCT116 (Supplementary Fig 5A) cells. In the CRC xenograft mouse models, SW480 cells and TGF-β1-pretreated SW480 cells were inoculated into nude mice, followed by treatment with adenovirus, and/or TGF-β1, and/or oxali­ platin, and other manipulations. Histone deacetylase HDAC4 induces chemoresistance via negatively regulating PI3K/AKT signaling pathway We have revealed that down-regulation of HDAC4 played a crucial role in TGF-β pathway-induced chemoresistance, and HDAC4 was also implicated in the TGF-β-associated epigenetic modification. Therefore, the correlation between epigenetic modification and chemoresistance needed to be clarified. Epigenetic modifications of histones occur at the genome level, and the “histone code”, as a widely accepted hypothesis, proposes that the combination of various epigenetic modifications of histones is involved in many biological processes [32]. We thus hy­ pothesized that the hyperacetylation of H3K9 and H3K18 induced by down-regulation of HDAC4 may lead to the aberrant regulation of some important biological signaling pathways, whereby results in chemo­ resistance to oxaliplatin. We have demonstrated that knockdown of HDAC4 increased acety­ lation levels of H3K9 and H3K18, meanwhile led to chemoresistance to oxaliplatin, suggesting that the target genes of H3K9ac and H3K18ac may be regulated by HDAC4, thereby involved in the role of chemo­ resistance to oxaliplatin. Increased levels of histone acetylation are generally associated with transcriptional activation [33], so a panel of genes that were upregulated in SW480 cells after TGF-β1 intervention were selected (Foldchange >0.4, p < 0.05); the target genes of H3K9ac and H3K18ac were obtained from our CHIP-seq data of histones. At last, the intersection of these upregulated genes and the target genes of H3K9ac and H3K18ac was selected, which was proposed to be regulated by HDAC4 (Fig. 5A and Table S6). Pathway enrichment analysis by DAVID showed that TGF-β signaling pathway was significantly activated, which is consistent with our expectation. As to the signaling pathways related to drug resistance, the anti-apoptotic pathway (PI3K/AKT) was also significantly activated (Fig. 5B). Numerous studies have confirmed its strong association with chemotherapy resistance [34], and the PI3K/AKT pathway inhibitor, Alpelisib, has been approved for the treatment of breast cancer [35], highlighting its clinical relevance. Thus we hypothesized that the epigenetic mechanism underlying chemoresistance induced by down-regulation of HDAC4 may be associated with the activation of the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway. To test the correlation between HDAC4 and PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, western blot assay was applied to investigate the expression of some key proteins (AKT and p-AKT) in PI3K/AKT signaling pathway. In this pathway, phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3 K) can phosphorylate PIP2, then AKT is phosphorylated into its activated form (p-AKT) by PIP3 [36]. Western blot analysis showed that p-AKT expression was significantly increased after knockdown of HDAC4, while the expression of total-AKT was not significantly different in the context of oxaliplatin intervention. In contrast, overexpression of HDAC4 inhibited p-AKT expression in SW480 cells and HCT116 cells (Fig. 5C). These findings indicated that the activation of PI3K/AKT signaling pathway was regulated by HDAC4. We further investigated the role of PI3K/AKT signaling pathway in TGF-β1-induced chemoresistance. Western blot revealed that administration of oxaliplatin to the TGF-β1-pretreated colorectal cancer cells increased the expression levels of p-AKT, sug­ gesting that this pathway was activated by TGF-β1. Conversely, over­ expression of HDAC4 in TGF-β1-pretreated colorectal cancer cells inhibited the activation of this signaling pathway (Fig. 5D), indicating that chemoresistance induced by TGF-β1 was closely associated with the activation of PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, which was regulated by the expression of HDAC4. Histone deacetylase HDAC4 is not involved in the regulation of EMT transition TGF-β1 is a strong inducer of EMT, and in our experiment, we also found the occurrence of EMT transition after TGF-β1 intervention in vitro. After the TGF-β1 intervention, SW480 changed to spindle-shaped. Western blot assay confirmed that the expression of epithelial marker E- cadherin and Occludin were downregulated, while mesenchymal markers and transcription factors (N-cadherin, VIM, ZEB1, and SNAI1) were upregulated (Supplementary Fig 6A&B). EMT transition has been considered to be closely related to drug resistance, so the relationship needed to be clarified between EMT transition and epigenetic modifi­ cation abnormalities caused by TGF-β1 intervention. Our experiment data confirmed that TGF-β1 stimulation led to down-regulation of HDAC4 expression and resulted in increased levels of histone acetyla­ tion. Although the cells showed chemoresistance to oxaliplatin after down-regulation of HDAC4 in SW480, however, we did not found obvious morphological changes; and the key epithelial marker CDH1 did not decrease after knockdown of HDAC4 in SW480 cells (Supplementary Fig 6C&D). These results suggest that HDAC4-mediated epigenetic modification may be independent of EMT transition induced by TGF-β1, which warrants further investigation. Discussion Global epigenetic abnormities have been widely analyzed in various cancers, and epigenetic mechanisms are demonstrated to play a key role in cell biology [15,37,38]. Previous studies mainly focused on the epi­ genome of the whole tumor or some particular cell populations. How­ ever, the epigenetic mechanisms underlying the concrete signaling pathway implicated in cancers are still unclear. We intended to inves­ tigate whether genome-wide epigenetic modifications were functionally present during the activation of the TGF-β signaling pathway in CRC. In our study, distinct epigenetic changes were found to occur in response to the activation of the TGF-β signaling pathway in CRC, which was characterized by the global hyperacetylation of H3K9 and H3K18. Mechanistically, these epigenetic changes were orchestrated by the histone deacetylase HDAC4, which functioned as a tumor suppressor through up-regulating the anti-apoptotic pathway (PI3K/AKT) to induce chemoresistance to oxaliplatin in CRC. Previous research indicates that the transformation induced by EMT is intricately linked to heightened tumor stemness and drug resistance [39,40]. Additionally, studies have revealed that TGF-β can induce chemotherapy resistance through other mechanisms, such as driving metabolic reprogramming of tumors [41], regulation of drug uptake and efflux [42], and inhibition of apoptosis [43]. However, its functionally epigenetic mechanism underlying these processes remains incompletely explored. Therefore, our study aims to illuminate a novel perspective on understanding chemoresistance in CRC. Histone modification plays a crucial role in transcriptional regula­ tion by influencing the chromatin conformation [44]. Hyperacetylation of histones typically contributes to transcriptional activation by loos­ ening the chromosome structure, facilitating the binding of transcription factors. Research by Molina et al. suggested that H3K9ac acts as a bar­ rier, preventing the spreading of heterochromatin and the inactivation of kinetochores at human centromeres [45]. Genome-wide analyses have demonstrated that H3K9ac and H3K14ac occupy the promoters of a majority of protein-coding genes in human embryonic stem cells [46]. Similarly, studies in acute myeloid leukemia and non-small cell lung cancer also showed that H3K9ac and H3K27ac localized on the pro­ moters of actively transcribed genes and led to carcinogenesis [47,48]. H3K9ac has also been implicated in drug resistance. Researches revealed a notable correlation between H3K9ac expression and drug resistance in glioblastoma, where increased promoter H3K9 acetylation (H3K9ac) was associated with elevated transcriptional levels of MGMT, contrib­ uting to glioblastoma resistance to TMZ [49,50]. These findings are consistent with our study, where we found that a high level of H3K9ac was necessary for TGF-β1-associated chemoresistance to oxaliplatin. H3k18ac was also related to transcription activation and was demonstrated to be implicated in thyroid tumorigenesis via the modu­ lation of DBC1/SIRT1 axis, wherein the transcription of DBC1, an endogenous inhibitor of SIRT1, was repressed by deacetylation of H3K18ac [51]. High expression of TGF-β1 has been linked to poor prognosis and drug resistance in CRC [17,20], and we have demon­ strated the global hyperacetylation of H3K18ac accompanied with the activation of TGF-β signaling pathway, although its epigenetic status has not been confirmed in the clinical specimens. Thus, we can infer that the hyperacetylation of H3K18 may be associated with the chemoresistance and the dismal outcome for CRC patients; however, there was still contradictory evidence for the prognostic value of epigenetic patterns in cancers. Seligson et al. found that lower global/cellular levels of H3K18ac and H3K4me2 predicted a higher risk of prostate cancer recurrence [52], and low level of H3K18ac was a sign of poor prognosis in pancreatic adenocarcinoma [53]. This discrepancy might mainly attribute to the intrinsic heterogeneity of tumors; in another way, what we have focused on was the global epigenome related to a biological signaling pathway in CRC, rather than the epigenetic abnormalities between different tumors. As to the prognostic value of epigenetic pat­ terns in CRC, including TGF-β signaling pathway-associated epigenome, still warrants further investigation. The hypothesis of “Histone code” implicated in the epigenetic regulation has been widely accepted, whereby the combination of various modifications to the histones leads to the modulation of different biological processes [54]. The histone acetylation is a dynamic and specific process, which is modulated by histone acetyltransferase (HAT) and histone deacetylase (HDAC) synergistically. However, the system­ atic understanding in this field has been hindered due to the complexity of the epigenetic mechanism. In our study, HDAC4 was demonstrated to play a crucial role in the regulation of H3K9ac and H3K18ac in the context of TGF-β signaling pathway, which might shed new light on the understanding of molecular mechanism associated with chemo­ resistance. The discrepancy in epigenetic regulation warrants further investigation. Our experimental data showed that HDAC4 was significantly down- regulated after the activation of the TGF-β signaling pathway. HDAC4 belongs to class II HDACs and has no deacetylase activity alone. Therefore, HDAC4 can be regarded as a regulatory factor, which regu­ lates transcription through bridging the enzymatically active SMRT/ N-CoR-HDAC3 complex [55]. Previous research suggests that HDAC4 is involved in various cancers, impacting cancer development and drug resistance by influencing the acetylation of histones or non-histone proteins, primarily transcription factors. For instance, a study has shown that HDAC4 can deacetylate glutaminase, promoting lung cancer progression [56]. In ovarian cancer, HDAC4 can induce resistance to platinum drugs by regulating acetylation modification of STAT1 [57]. Additionally, HDAC4 exerts an effect of activating p38 MAPK signaling through the autophagic degradation of MEKK3, facilitating gastric cancer growth and metastasis [58] Importantly, the chemoresistance associated with HDAC4 showed significant tumor specificity. In colon cancer, knockdown of HDAC4 induced chemoresistance to 5-fluorouracil [59]; Wei et al. also found that knockdown of HDAC4 inhibited melatonin-induced apoptosis in colorectal cancer cells [60]. These studies were consistent with our experimental results. We have demonstrated that low expression of X. Tian et al. We also found that the recurrent CRC patients had relatively low expression of HDAC4. Moreover, patients with a low expression of HDAC4 were prone to have poor relapse-free survival by analyzing two GEO data. However, in other tumors, such as ovarian cancer, lung cancer, etc., drug resistance was closely related to the high expression of HDAC4. Knockdown of HDAC4 increased the sensitivity to chemother­ apeutic agents [57,61]. Significant differences in the role of HDAC4 among different tumors suggest that its regulatory mechanism might be context-dependent; so it is of paramount clinical significance to further dissect its molecular mechanism in the context of current epigenetic therapy. In our study, HDAC4 was found to be downregulated after the acti­ vation of the TGF-β signaling pathway and then activated the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway via epigenetic modification. The role of anti- apoptosis related to PI3K/AKT signaling pathway was one of the important mechanisms underlying chemoresistance [34,62]. The TGF-β signaling pathway is closely related to the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway with complex mutual interactions. On the one hand, TGF-β signaling pathway activation can motivate the PI3K-AKT pathway directly or indirectly. Studies revealed that TGF-β could induce AKT phosphoryla­ tion, and then downstream effector molecules of AKT, were all activated independent of the classical SMAD pathway [63]. Besides the direct interaction, the PI3K-AKT pathway could also be activated by inhibiting the expression of the tumor suppressor gene PTEN in a microRNA-dependent manner after the activation of the TGF-β pathway [64,65]. On the other hand, TGF-β pathway can be regulated by PI3K-AKT pathway as well. AKT could directly interact with SMAD3 to block phosphorylation of SMAD3, thereby inhibiting the apoptosis induced by TGF-β/SMAD pathway [65]. The epigenetic regulatory mechanism mediated by HDAC4 has been identified through our study between TGF-β and PI3K/AKT pathway, which will contribute to further exploration of the crosstalk between these two biological signaling pathways. Recent studies have highlighted the intricate relationship between TGF-β and immunity. TGF-β plays a regulatory role in the development, activation, proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis of T cells, exerting control over nearly every stage of the immune response elicited by tumors [66,67]. Moreover, TGF-β has been shown to induce immune escape in tumors by influencing tumor dendritic cells [18]. Targeting TGF-β presents an opportunity to overcome immune evasion in tumors [68]. In the future, there is a need for further investigation into the epigenetic mechanisms of TGF-β in regulating tumor immune response Our research mainly focuses on the epigenetic regulatory mechanism associated with TGF-β pathway (Fig 5E), and we have made a pre­ liminary exploration on drug resistance in TGF-β-related CRC patients, hoping to provide a new perspective and theoretical basis for the treatment of CRC.                       Guide

                      You do not type anything

                      Select the words in the Methods text, then press Use highlighted text. The exact words you selected are what gets recorded.

                      Typing is what this design removes. Given a sentence like the one below, one person types RIPA, another types the whole parenthesis, a third types the clause. All three read the paper correctly and disagree in the data. Selecting removes that entirely.

                      The cell total protein was isolated using RIPA (Applygen Technologies Inc., Beijing, China). Protease inhibitor (CWBIO, Shanghai, China) was added into the RIPA at a ratio of 1:100.

                      For lysis buffer there, mark just RIPA, the first time it appears.

                      How much to select

                      The shortest phrase that states the detail. Not the whole sentence when three words will do, and not the vendor, city or catalog number unless the question asks for them.

                      Every question shows a worked example under What to mark. Follow it rather than your instinct.

                      When a paper gives several

                      Many papers list a table of antibodies with eight RRIDs, eight catalog numbers and several vendors. Mark them all. Press Use highlighted text once per value and they stack up in a numbered list. Press Done, next when the list is complete.

                      If they sit in a contiguous table or a run-on list, selecting the whole block in one go is fine and much faster. You are marking where the evidence is, not counting.

                      The same value repeated does not need marking twice. A paper that uses 1:1000 for six antibodies has one dilution, not six.

                      If you stop before you have them all, tick 'There are more of these than I marked'. That box matters more than it looks: without it, someone who stopped at five of eight cannot be told apart from someone who found everything, and the three they skipped would count against the extractor as mistakes it did not make.

                      What counts

                      Judge only what is written. Not what the authors almost certainly did, and not what is standard practice in the field.

                      Judge the text shown. The page shows the Methods section, and sometimes figure legends or the full text where no Methods heading was found. A detail in the text shown counts, legend included. A detail found only in a legend or supplementary file that is not shown is Not stated.

                      Unclear is a real answer. It is set aside rather than counted either way, so use it when you are genuinely stuck rather than guessing.

                      The shaded sentences

                      The faint tint marks the western blot section: sentences that name the blot, the gel, the membrane or its detection. It is the same for every question on a paper. Blot section jumps between its blocks. Other assays are left untinted, but an item can still be stated elsewhere, for example in a figure legend.

                      The warmer marks are the sentences most likely to hold the answer to the open question, and Prev and Next step between them.

                      Both are search aids and nothing more. Fixed keyword rules pick them; they are never told the answer, they sometimes shade irrelevant sentences and sometimes miss the right one. Read around them, and judge only the text.

                      Blinded quantification statedA statement that the blot's band quantification or image analysis was done blind to group or treatment. Blinding of another assay, of animal allocation, or of the study as a whole without naming the blot analysis does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the statement. In densitometry was performed by an investigator blinded to treatment group mark the whole phrase. Nothing to mark in histology was scored by a blinded pathologist.
                      Denaturation time and temperatureThe temperature and time at which the blot's samples were heated before loading, both stated; 'boiled' states the temperature, so 'boiled for 5 min' counts. 'Boiled' or 'heated' without a time does not count, and neither does heating in elution buffer. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the temperature and time together. In heated at 95 °C for 5 min or boiled for 5 min mark the whole phrase. Nothing to mark in boiled in Laemmli buffer.
                      Substrate incubation timeHow long the membrane was incubated in the detection substrate before imaging. An exposure or imaging time does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the time. In incubated with ECL for 1 min mark 1 min. Nothing to mark in an exposure time.
                      Loading control validatedA statement that the loading control or normalizing signal was checked, shown or tested to be unchanged across the conditions compared. Naming the control, or normalizing to it, alone does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the statement. In GAPDH did not differ between groups (Supplementary Fig. S2) mark the whole phrase. Nothing to mark in normalized to GAPDH.
                      Normalization statedHow the blot's band intensities were normalized, with the reference named: to a loading control, to total protein, or phospho to total ('normalized to beta-actin' is enough; the arithmetic need not be spelled out). Expressing values relative to a control group alone does not count, and neither does naming a loading control without saying the bands were normalized to it, a statistics statement that names no assay, or a pooled reference sample used only to compare blots (between-blot normalization). Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the phrase that states the calculation. In band intensities were normalized to beta-actin and expressed relative to control mark normalized to beta-actin. Nothing to mark in quantitated as percent of DMSO, GAPDH was used as a loading control, or gene expression was normalized to GAPDH (qPCR).
                      Lysate storage statedHow the blot's lysates or protein extracts were stored before loading: a storage temperature, with or without a duration ('lysates were stored at -80 °C until use'). Storage of tissue, cells, blood or other samples before lysis, and storage of antibodies or reagents, does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the words that state the storage. In the supernatant was aliquoted and stored at -80 °C mark stored at -80 °C. Nothing to mark in tissues were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C.
                      Antibody validation statedA statement that an antibody's specificity was checked experimentally: knockdown or knockout, a blocking peptide, overexpression, or a positive and a negative control sample stated as checking the antibody's specificity. Routine assay controls, normal serum or no-primary substitutions, isotype controls, a single band at the expected size, a vendor's claim, or a citation alone does not count. Counts for an antibody used in any assay, not only the blot.
                      Mark: Mark the statement. In antibody specificity was confirmed in PAX6 knockdown cells mark the whole phrase. Nothing to mark in a single band was detected at the expected molecular weight, the primary antibody was replaced with normal rabbit serum as a negative control, or validated by the manufacturer.
                      Signal within dynamic range statedA statement that the blot's signal was checked to lie within the linear or dynamic range, or to be unsaturated: a loading or dilution series, an unsaturated-exposure check, or the imager's saturation indicator. 'Optimal exposure', or an exposure time alone, does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the statement. In exposures were chosen so that no band was saturated mark the whole phrase. Nothing to mark in the optimal exposure was used.
