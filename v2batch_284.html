What counts as a western blot. Western blot means proteins separated on a gel, transferred to a membrane and detected with an antibody. An OxyBlot counts. A dot blot or slot blot, of RNA or of protein, does not; neither does a capillary system such as Wes or Jess, which has no membrane, or a membrane probed only with streptavidin or a lectin, which uses no antibody. Items marked 'Western blot only' do not count the same thing described for another assay in the paper: qPCR, immunofluorescence, IHC, flow cytometry, ELISA, mass spectrometry, or a gel that was stained rather than blotted. Judge the text the page shows, including any figure legend printed in it; a detail found only in a legend or supplement that is not shown is not stated. A method given only by citation ('as previously described [12]') is not stated: mark only what the text itself says. The Shortcut citation item is the exception: it records the citation phrase itself. Some PDFs drop the micro sign or print it as m ('50 g', '25 mg' for micrograms); rule on the intended unit.

Every question carries its own What to mark rule with a worked example. Follow it rather than your instinct: consistency between reviewers matters more here than any single choice. Full rules under Guide.

Transforming growth factor beta (TGFβ) is a pro-fibrotic cytokine that is central to fibrosis. In one study, overexpression of TOLLIP in human cell lines (HepG2 and HEK293T) impaired TGFβ signaling through modulation of SMAD7, an inhibitory SMAD in the canon- ical TGFβ signaling pathway, and trafficking of TGFβ type I receptor.7 Thus, TOLLIP appears to dampen TGFβ signal- ing in vitro. How TOLLIP affects this signaling cascade in primary mouse lung fibroblasts (MLFs), the cells respon- sible for matrix deposition and architectural distortion in lung fibrosis, and whether TOLLIP deficiency affects out- comes in experimental fibrosis remain unknown. In this study, we utilized transgenic Tollip knockout mice (Tollip-/-) to examine the effect of TOLLIP deficiency in an experimental model of lung fibrosis. We hypothesized that if TOLLIP negatively regulates TGFβ signaling, then mouse lung fibroblasts (MLFs) from Tollip-/- mice would exhibit enhanced transcriptional and functional responses to TGFβ stimulation in vitro. Expression of TOLLIP has been shown to be lower in lung tissues from IPF subjects compared to controls.6 A recent obser- vational study on chronic hypersensitivity pneumonitis found that subjects with the GG genotype at the rs5743899 locus in the TOLLIP gene experienced more rapid clini- cal decline as defined by change in forced vital capacity over time.16 The GG genotype is associated with lower transcription and translation of TOLLIP, as well as higher phospho-SMAD2 in lung tissues, suggesting enhanced TGFβ signaling in these subjects. Despite the association between reduced TOLLIP expression and lung fibrosis in population studies, mechanistic insight remains sparse in the literature. In this study, we hypothesized that TOLLIP defi- ciency leads to enhanced TGFβ signaling in mouse lung fibroblasts and increased fibrosis in the bleomycin injury model. We confirmed enhanced response to TGFβ signal- ing in Tollip-/- MLFs, demonstrating increased phos- phorylation of canonical SMAD pathway and functional differences including migration and matrix invasiveness after activation with TGFβ. Acta2 expression was signifi- cantly increased in TGFβ-treated Tollip-/- MLFs com- pared to WT by qPCR, though the upregulation of Col1a1 was not different between the genotypes. Expression of Fn and Col3a1 was not increased in stimulated MLFs of either genotype, although transcriptional response in these genes may not be apparent at 24 h. These results are consistent with the transcriptional profiling of MLFs by RNA-seq following TGFβ treatment for 24 h. In the differ- entially expressed genes analysis, Acta2 and Col1a1 were both significantly upregulated compared to unstimulated controls, whereas Col3a1 and Fn1 were not (significance defined by a cutoff of fold change >2, false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05). Given the strong profibrotic effect of TGFβ on cul- tured fibroblasts, we anticipated that stimulated WT and Tollip-/- MLFs would undergo myofibroblastic transcrip- tional changes as reflected in the RNA-seq analysis. Both WT and Tollip-/- MLFs upregulated matrix-associated and contractile genes including Col12a1, Col11a1, Actg2, Timp1, Myh11, Acta2, Serpinh1, Col1a1, and Lox in re- sponse to TGFβ stimulation. While both genotypes shared many myofibroblast features with stimulation, Tollip-/- MLFs maintained higher numbers of differentially reg- ulated genes compared to WT MLFs at 24 h. Together with the functional responses to TGFβ stimulation and increased pSMAD2 signals in Tollip-/- fibroblasts, our study provides supportive evidence for a regulatory role of TOLLIP in TGFβ signaling in lung fibroblasts. Limited evidence in human cell lines suggests TOLLIP downregulates SMAD2/3 signaling, thereby acting as a “brake” against unchecked and persistent TGFβ stimula- tion. TGFβ receptors reside on cell membranes and are constantly recycled.17 Receptor binding to TGFβ leads to heterodimerization of TGFβ receptors I and II and canonical SMAD and noncanonical pathway signaling downstream to regulate the expression of TGFβ respon- sive genes.17,18 Signaling through TGFβ receptors is mod- ulated by receptor availability.19 Ubiquination of TGFβ receptor I upon binding to its ligand targets the receptor for intracellular degradation.20,21 In a study using human epithelial tumor cell lines (HepG2 and HEK293T), Zhu and colleagues showed that human TOLLIP, in conjunc- tion with SMAD7, interacts with ubiquinated TGFβ recep- tor I through the ubiquitin binding domain on TOLLIP.7 Overexpression of human TOLLIP accelerated degrada- tion of TGFβ receptor I, thereby dampening the effect of TGFβ. Modulation of TGFβ signaling in primary MLFs may occur through similar receptor-level controls where 24 | CHOW et al. Tollip-/- mice lack the “braking” effect by TOLLIP. Tollip-/- lung homogenates from injured lungs collected at Day 21 exhibited greater p-SMAD2 levels similar to MLFs. Furthermore, protein expression of TGFβR1 was higher in Tollip-/-, suggesting impaired negative regula- tion of TGFβ signaling has mechanistic relevance in this in vivo model. While not extensively reported in the literature, weight- based bleomycin dosing leads to greater lung injury in male mice than in female mice. In our study, male mice experienced lower survival with standard dosing (1.3 U/ kg) compared to female mice. These results are consistent with previously reported sex dimorphism in bleomycin-in- duced lung injury in which male mice exhibit greater lung injury compared to female mice when administered a weight-based bleomycin regimen.22 To ensure adequate survival of male mice to Day 21 and to ensure similar lung injury between the sexes, we repeated the study with a sex- and weight-based (modified) regimen. Neither the standard nor the modified bleomycin regi- men led to a significant difference in lung fibrosis, despite the enhanced response of Tollip-/- MLFs to TGFβ. One potential confounder is the well-recognized modulatory role of TOLLIP in acute inflammation through IL-1R and TLRs,2 which may bias outcomes in fibrosis. In our study, we examined acute inflammatory responses (Day 7) and did not find significant differences in markers of acute injury including BALF WBC and total protein. Thus, our findings in fibrosis were not likely influenced by early dif- ferences in acute lung injury. Interestingly, TOLLIP deficiency affected survival and resolution in this model. The survival difference was ap- parent in the standard dosing (1.3 U/kg). Tollip-/- male mice exhibited markedly lower survival than WT male mice at this dose. While the modified dose of bleomycin (1 U/kg for male, 1.8 U/kg for female) did not lead to a survival difference between genotypes, Tollip-/- mice nevertheless showed persistent and significant increase in weight loss and BALF total protein compared to WT at Day 14, suggesting impaired early resolution. The mechanism for the survival difference (at the stan- dard bleomycin dose) and the impaired early resolution (at the adjusted dose) is incompletely understood. Possible explanations include greater susceptibility to oxidative stress and altered repair functions in Tollip-/- alveolar epithelial cells. Although we did not test for these pos- sibilities in this study, there is evidence in the literature to suggest these processes may have contributed to in- creased bleomycin susceptibility in Tollip-/- animals.6 Furthermore, how TOLLIP deficiency alters the composi- tion and function of monocyte and macrophage subpop- ulations in the bleomycin model remains unknown. Our study further highlights the complex mechanistic roles TOLLIP may play in lung repair through modulation of the TGFβ pathway, in MLFs and likely in other cellular populations critical to normal lung repair including epi- thelial cells, monocytes, and macrophages. Investigation of these mechanistic roles will require further studies em- ploying lineage-specific deletion of TOLLIP in relevant injury models. In summary, TOLLIP deficiency enhances TGFβ sig- naling in MLFs. Tollip-/- mice experience lower survival and impaired resolution of lung injury in the bleomycin model. Despite these differences, organ fibrosis is not al- tered. Future directions will focus on the role of TOLLIP in regulating resistance to oxidative stress in lung epithelial cells and how TOLLIP deficiency affects repair by differ- ent cellular populations of the lung including alveolar ep- ithelial cells (type 1 and 2), monocytes, and macrophages.                       Guide

                      You do not type anything

                      Select the words in the Methods text, then press Use highlighted text. The exact words you selected are what gets recorded.

                      Typing is what this design removes. Given a sentence like the one below, one person types RIPA, another types the whole parenthesis, a third types the clause. All three read the paper correctly and disagree in the data. Selecting removes that entirely.

                      The cell total protein was isolated using RIPA (Applygen Technologies Inc., Beijing, China). Protease inhibitor (CWBIO, Shanghai, China) was added into the RIPA at a ratio of 1:100.

                      For lysis buffer there, mark just RIPA, the first time it appears.

                      How much to select

                      The shortest phrase that states the detail. Not the whole sentence when three words will do, and not the vendor, city or catalog number unless the question asks for them.

                      Every question shows a worked example under What to mark. Follow it rather than your instinct.

                      When a paper gives several

                      Many papers list a table of antibodies with eight RRIDs, eight catalog numbers and several vendors. Mark them all. Press Use highlighted text once per value and they stack up in a numbered list. Press Done, next when the list is complete.

                      If they sit in a contiguous table or a run-on list, selecting the whole block in one go is fine and much faster. You are marking where the evidence is, not counting.

                      The same value repeated does not need marking twice. A paper that uses 1:1000 for six antibodies has one dilution, not six.

                      If you stop before you have them all, tick 'There are more of these than I marked'. That box matters more than it looks: without it, someone who stopped at five of eight cannot be told apart from someone who found everything, and the three they skipped would count against the extractor as mistakes it did not make.

                      What counts

                      Judge only what is written. Not what the authors almost certainly did, and not what is standard practice in the field.

                      Judge the text shown. The page shows the Methods section, and sometimes figure legends or the full text where no Methods heading was found. A detail in the text shown counts, legend included. A detail found only in a legend or supplementary file that is not shown is Not stated.

                      Unclear is a real answer. It is set aside rather than counted either way, so use it when you are genuinely stuck rather than guessing.

                      The shaded sentences

                      The faint tint marks the western blot section: sentences that name the blot, the gel, the membrane or its detection. It is the same for every question on a paper. Blot section jumps between its blocks. Other assays are left untinted, but an item can still be stated elsewhere, for example in a figure legend.

                      The warmer marks are the sentences most likely to hold the answer to the open question, and Prev and Next step between them.

                      Both are search aids and nothing more. Fixed keyword rules pick them; they are never told the answer, they sometimes shade irrelevant sentences and sometimes miss the right one. Read around them, and judge only the text.
