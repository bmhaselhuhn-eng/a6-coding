What counts as a western blot. Western blot means proteins separated on a gel, transferred to a membrane and detected with an antibody. An OxyBlot counts. A dot blot or slot blot, of RNA or of protein, does not; neither does a capillary system such as Wes or Jess, which has no membrane, or a membrane probed only with streptavidin or a lectin, which uses no antibody. Items marked 'Western blot only' do not count the same thing described for another assay in the paper: qPCR, immunofluorescence, IHC, flow cytometry, ELISA, mass spectrometry, or a gel that was stained rather than blotted. Judge the text the page shows, including any figure legend printed in it; a detail found only in a legend or supplement that is not shown is not stated. A method given only by citation ('as previously described [12]') is not stated: mark only what the text itself says. The Shortcut citation item is the exception: it records the citation phrase itself. Some PDFs drop the micro sign or print it as m ('50 g', '25 mg' for micrograms); rule on the intended unit.

Every question carries its own What to mark rule with a worked example. Follow it rather than your instinct: consistency between reviewers matters more here than any single choice. Full rules under Guide.

Discussion In addition to the effect of asbestos fiber exposure on the passive membrane properties (Rm and RP) and the increase in the outward current amplitude induced by depolarizing steps already described in our previous studies [3,4], here we identify TMEM16A channels as a new molecular player in the biological effect of asbestos fibers in Xenopus oocytes. Our conclusion arose from data showing that Croc exposure caused an alteration of the Cl-membrane permeability and a strong reduction in the effect of the Croc treatment Membranes 2023, 13, 180 11 of 15 after replacing external Cl-ion with aspartate. Moreover, our experiments revealed a potentiation of the effect of the Croc treatment by increasing the [Ca2+]e from 1.8 to 11 mM and by stepping the voltages of the membrane from a more hyperpolarized level Vh (-80 mV) and the blockage of the Croc-sensitive currents by the specific TMEM16A channel antagonists MONNA and Ani9. Concerning the role of TMEM16A in mediating the effect of the asbestos fiber exposure on the passive oocyte membrane properties, here we observed that the reduction in Rm caused by the Croc-treatment was partially restored by the specific TMEM16A antagonists suggesting a “tonic” activation of the TMEM16A channels following the Croc-treatment. Accordingly, the overexpression of TMEM16A channels has already been demonstrated to lead to a reduction in the Rm in Xenopus oocytes [8]. At least two types of CaCCs currents have been described in Xenopus oocytes. The first is induced by a “standard” protocol (stepping positive from Vh = -40 mV in normal Ringer) [6] and a second (transient) outward current elicited from a more hyperpolarized Vh (-80 mV or -100 mV) as well by increasing the [Ca2+]e [5,29]. Both currents were ob- served in our experimental conditions (Figures 1a and 3a). Xenopus oocytes have long been considered a convenient model for studying CaCCs, in view of these channels being the predominant type natively expressed in these cells. The expression of Xenopus TMEM16A in Axolotl oocytes produces evoked outward currents with multiple components [6], sup- porting the idea that CaCC currents recorded in Xenopus oocytes are also likely to be carried by the same type of channels [23]. At least two “sources” could be the responsible for any variation of the [Ca2+]i fol- lowing step depolarization: Ca2+ released from internal stores (in oocytes, mostly the IP3-sensitive stores) or the Ca2+ influx from the extracellular space. Here, we found that in Ca2+-free Ringer solution, the [Ca2+]i of Croc-treated cells was similar to Ctrl cells. On the other hand, in normal condition, the Croc exposure increased the [Ca2+]i with respect to non-treated cells. This finding supports the Ca2+ influx from the extracellular space as a predominant source of Ca2+ induced by the asbestos fibers. Additionally, considering our previous studies [3,4], we suggest at least three possible mechanisms by which Croc could increase the Ca2+ influx and thus the “resting” [Ca2+]i (as we observed flourometrically) and TMEM16A activation in Xenopus oocytes; 1) by increasing the Ca2+ influx through VOCC; 2) by forming membrane “pores” permeable to Ca2+ [3]; 3) or by modulating Ca2+ currents from some unknown leak channels [27,30]. All of them could increase the [Ca2+]i and thus induce a tonic activation of the TMEM16A channel activity, altering in this way the passive membrane properties of the cells. One more possibility must be also mentioned. Many proteins, with different structures, molecular weight and isoelectric point are adsorbed with a specific profile by specific asbestos fibers, suggesting a specific interaction [31]. Several authors have investigated the extent of adsorbed proteins and the nature of these, but only rarely have analyzed the function of the adsorbed protein. Borelli et al. (2007) [32] and Pascolo et al. (2015) [33], showed that the so-called asbestos “body” consists of the asbestos fiber covered by ferritin which was shown to become unfolded, and Borelli et al. (2018) [34] reported that mast cell enzymes undergo a significant activation following asbestos binding. These findings suggest that after asbestos fiber adsorption, the target protein can be modified both in its structure and function. Our Western blotting analysis showed that Croc binds TMEM16A proteins more than the other fibers tested. Therefore, we could speculate an enhancing channel activity effect mediated by a direct interaction of Croc with the TMEM16A protein. In support of this, Woll who just poorly pulled-down the channel, did not elicit any electrical effect on Xenopus cell membranes [3] nor has it been considered a carcinogen [35] suggesting a cause-and-effect relationship between electrical changes and carcinogenesis. Chry also bound TMEM16A, even though to a much lesser extent as compared to Croc, and its effect on the channel activity is currently under investigation. Interestingly, the asbestos fiber absorption to TMEM16A was detected also by incu- bating an extract of a membrane-rich fraction obtained from a human mesothelial cell Membranes 2023, 13, 180 12 of 15 line (MeT5a). While oocytes express only one isoform of TMEM16A, two isoforms were expressed by MeT5a cells and the asbestos fibers mainly absorbed the lower isoform. Ex- plaining why one human isoform shows a higher affinity for Croc fibers was beyond the aim of the present study; however, the results indicated a possible direct TMEM16A-fiber interaction in Xenopus oocytes as well as in human cells. If this is the case, this interaction could also modulate some TMEM16A channel properties, such as the Ca2+-and/or voltage sensitivity. This aspect deserves to be investigated in the future. The evidence for TMEM16A gene amplification and over expression in several types of tumors is well consolidated [9,13-17]. The relationship between TMEM16A expres- Membranes 2023, 13, 180 13 of 15 sion/activity and tumor growth/invasion has been demonstrated in human prostate carci- noma cells both in vivo and in vitro [36,37]. The hypothesis of the TMEM16A channel as an indirect or even direct membrane molecular player in the asbestos-associated pathologies in humans has been proposed by a wide range of experimental work and stressed in excellent reviews. In addition, also our bioinformatic analysis (see Supplementary Figures S2 and S3, [38-42]) suggests that this molecule can play a key role in the development of asbestos- related tumors as well (i.e., head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, lung adenocarcinoma, lung squamous cell carcinoma) and that the overexpression of TMEM16A channels could represent a good prognostic factor. Although the mechanism through which TMEM16A exerts the tumorigenic activity is not completely clear, recent studies suggest that it interacts with the Ezrin-Radixin-Moesin (ERM) network in human embryonic kidney (HEK) cells [43]. Considering that the ERM network is an intermediate in the connection between the actin cytoskeleton and the plasma membrane, the TMEM16A-ERM network may regulate cancer cell migration, invasion or adhesion [44]. Interestingly, the overexpression of TMEM16A was also reported to affect the morphology of the cell membrane, via the ERM network in Xenopus oocytes [36] and we have previously shown that Xenopus oocyte membranes undergo morphological changes following asbestos fiber exposure [3]. 5. Conclusions Taking advantage of the Xenopus oocyte model, the present study strongly implicates TMEM16A channels as key molecular participants in the Croc asbestos-mediated effects on cell membranes. The alteration of Ca2+ homeostasis is a well-known tumorigenic hallmark in lung cancer [45]. Our findings suggest a cross-talk between the [Ca2+]i variation observed during asbestos exposure and the activation of TMEM16A channels. Asbestos fibers can also affect cell function both directly via ROS production, induction of membrane lesions [34] and following induction of iron overload, induction of ferroptosis and mutagenicity [46,47]. Activation of TMEM16A can strongly contribute to the tumor promoting activity of asbestos by enhancing cell motility and growth. Consequently, TMEM16A channels may have a role in tumorigenesis in the pathophysiology of asbestos-related human tumors. In this perspective, we think that the use of TMEM16A channels as a new therapeutic target and/or prognostic marker in the management of asbestos-related diseases would merit future attention. Each type of asbestos may be involved in tumorigenesis. However, the role of Chry in this sense is controversial with respect to that of Croc [48]. Therefore, a relationship between electrophysiological changes induced in our model and tumorigenesis is presently difficult to draw. The differential contribution of these fibers in affecting the electrophysiological properties of Xenopus oocytes will be addressed in a future dedicated research program.                       Guide

                      You do not type anything

                      Select the words in the Methods text, then press Use highlighted text. The exact words you selected are what gets recorded.

                      Typing is what this design removes. Given a sentence like the one below, one person types RIPA, another types the whole parenthesis, a third types the clause. All three read the paper correctly and disagree in the data. Selecting removes that entirely.

                      The cell total protein was isolated using RIPA (Applygen Technologies Inc., Beijing, China). Protease inhibitor (CWBIO, Shanghai, China) was added into the RIPA at a ratio of 1:100.

                      For lysis buffer there, mark just RIPA, the first time it appears.

                      How much to select

                      The shortest phrase that states the detail. Not the whole sentence when three words will do, and not the vendor, city or catalog number unless the question asks for them.

                      Every question shows a worked example under What to mark. Follow it rather than your instinct.

                      When a paper gives several

                      Many papers list a table of antibodies with eight RRIDs, eight catalog numbers and several vendors. Mark them all. Press Use highlighted text once per value and they stack up in a numbered list. Press Done, next when the list is complete.

                      If they sit in a contiguous table or a run-on list, selecting the whole block in one go is fine and much faster. You are marking where the evidence is, not counting.

                      The same value repeated does not need marking twice. A paper that uses 1:1000 for six antibodies has one dilution, not six.

                      If you stop before you have them all, tick 'There are more of these than I marked'. That box matters more than it looks: without it, someone who stopped at five of eight cannot be told apart from someone who found everything, and the three they skipped would count against the extractor as mistakes it did not make.

                      What counts

                      Judge only what is written. Not what the authors almost certainly did, and not what is standard practice in the field.

                      Judge the text shown. The page shows the Methods section, and sometimes figure legends or the full text where no Methods heading was found. A detail in the text shown counts, legend included. A detail found only in a legend or supplementary file that is not shown is Not stated.

                      Unclear is a real answer. It is set aside rather than counted either way, so use it when you are genuinely stuck rather than guessing.

                      The shaded sentences

                      The faint tint marks the western blot section: sentences that name the blot, the gel, the membrane or its detection. It is the same for every question on a paper. Blot section jumps between its blocks. Other assays are left untinted, but an item can still be stated elsewhere, for example in a figure legend.

                      The warmer marks are the sentences most likely to hold the answer to the open question, and Prev and Next step between them.

                      Both are search aids and nothing more. Fixed keyword rules pick them; they are never told the answer, they sometimes shade irrelevant sentences and sometimes miss the right one. Read around them, and judge only the text.

                      Every question, in one place

                      Signal within dynamic range statedA statement that the blot's signal was checked to lie within the linear or dynamic range, or to be unsaturated: a loading or dilution series, an unsaturated-exposure check, or the imager's saturation indicator. 'Optimal exposure', or an exposure time alone, does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the statement. In exposures were chosen so that no band was saturated mark the whole phrase. Nothing to mark in the optimal exposure was used.
                      Stripping and reprobing statedA statement that a membrane was stripped and reprobed, with or without the method. 'Reprobed' with no stripping stated does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the statement. In membranes were stripped with 0.2 M NaOH and reprobed mark the whole phrase.
                      Lysate storage statedHow the blot's lysates or protein extracts were stored before loading: a storage temperature, with or without a duration ('lysates were stored at -80 °C until use'). Storage of tissue, cells, blood or other samples before lysis, and storage of antibodies or reagents, does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the words that state the storage. In the supernatant was aliquoted and stored at -80 °C mark stored at -80 °C. Nothing to mark in tissues were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C.
                      Transfer conditionsThe voltage or current, the time, or the temperature of the transfer. Any one counts. The transfer method alone ('wet transfer') does not. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the conditions. In transferred at 100 V for 1 h at 4 °C mark the whole phrase.
                      Lysis buffer compositionThe lysis buffer's recipe, or the product used. A recipe with at least one component of the base buffer and its concentration counts, and so does a product identified by brand name, vendor or catalog number. A generic name alone, such as 'RIPA buffer', does not count, because RIPA recipes differ, and neither do protease or phosphatase inhibitors added to it, which are their own item. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the recipe, or the product with what identifies it. In RIPA (50 mM Tris pH 7.4, 150 mM NaCl, 1% NP-40, 0.5% deoxycholate, 0.1% SDS) mark the parenthesis; in RIPA (Applygen Technologies) mark the whole phrase. Nothing to mark in lysed in RIPA buffer.
                      Blocking agent sourceThe supplier, product or grade of the blocking agent: 'BSA (Sigma, fraction V)', 'non-fat dry milk (Bio-Rad)', or a named commercial blocking buffer such as Intercept Blocking Buffer. The agent or its percentage alone does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the supplier, product or grade words. In blocked in 5% BSA (Sigma-Aldrich) mark Sigma-Aldrich. Nothing to mark in blocked in 5% non-fat milk.
                      Molecular weight markerA molecular weight marker or protein ladder stated as run on the blot's gel, named or not, including one named in the SDS-PAGE paragraph for the gel that was blotted. A band size or predicted weight alone, or troubleshooting advice, does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the marker name, or the words stating a marker was run. In alongside PageRuler Prestained Protein Ladder mark PageRuler Prestained Protein Ladder. Nothing to mark in a band at 53 kDa or in an antibody table listing predicted sizes.
                      Antibody concentration by massAn antibody working concentration in mass per volume (for example 0.5 ug/mL). A dilution ratio alone does not count. Counts for an antibody used in any assay, not only the blot.
                      Mark: Mark each distinct concentration once. In anti-p53 at 1 ug/mL mark 1 ug/mL.
                      Gel and running buffer systemThe gel chemistry or the running buffer, either one (Tris-glycine, Bis-Tris, MOPS or MES, Tris-acetate, Tricine), or a named precast gel product. 'SDS-PAGE' alone, or a gel percentage alone, does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the words naming the system or product. In NuPAGE 4-12% Bis-Tris gels in MES running buffer mark the whole phrase.

                      Ask about a rule

                      This answers questions about what a rule means, nothing else. It is never given the paper, it cannot see what you are reading, and it will refuse if you paste text and ask what the answer is. Deciding what a paper says is the whole job being measured here, so it has to stay yours.

                      Questions are logged with your initials, so ask freely and openly.
