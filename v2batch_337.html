What counts as a western blot. Western blot means proteins separated on a gel, transferred to a membrane and detected with an antibody. An OxyBlot counts. A dot blot or slot blot, of RNA or of protein, does not; neither does a capillary system such as Wes or Jess, which has no membrane, or a membrane probed only with streptavidin or a lectin, which uses no antibody. Items marked 'Western blot only' do not count the same thing described for another assay in the paper: qPCR, immunofluorescence, IHC, flow cytometry, ELISA, mass spectrometry, or a gel that was stained rather than blotted. Judge the text the page shows, including any figure legend printed in it; a detail found only in a legend or supplement that is not shown is not stated. A method given only by citation ('as previously described [12]') is not stated: mark only what the text itself says. The Shortcut citation item is the exception: it records the citation phrase itself. Some PDFs drop the micro sign or print it as m ('50 g', '25 mg' for micrograms); rule on the intended unit.

Every question carries its own What to mark rule with a worked example. Follow it rather than your instinct: consistency between reviewers matters more here than any single choice. Full rules under Guide.

Nemosis is a newly discovered way of fibroblast activation. Many investigations have reported that this type of dental fibroblast activation (nemosis) may be involved in pathophysiological conditions in dental pulp. Our experimental SEM results showed that without external stimulation, only cellecell contact of human DPFs could induce cell membrane damage. Clustering DPFs could express the angiogenic promoting factor vascular endothelial-derived growth factor (VEGF), growth factor hepatocyte growth factor/scatter factor (HGF/SF) and chemokines (CCL20 and CXCL8) [7]. The implication of soluble factors secreted by clustering fibroblasts is significant. Prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) is the most abundantly detected prostaglandin (PG) in clustering fibroblasts. It is important in modulating the inflammatory and healing processes in pulp inflammation [28]. PGE2 is bio- synthesized from arachidonic acid by cyclooxygenases (COXs) as the rate-limiting enzymes. At present, there are three isozymes of COX, namely, COX-1, COX-2 and COX-3. Our results suggest that NLRP3 and AIM2 inflammasome-dependent sterile inflammation can be induced in clustering human DPFs. Inflammasomes can be activated without bacteria stimulation in three-dimensional cultured human DPFs spheroids. This is a new idea different from the traditional relationship in which inflam- masome activation can be induced by microbial infection in DPFs [18,19]. Similarly, NLRP3 inflammasome-dependent sterile inflam- mation was also found in dead odontoblasts [35]. Overall, inflammasome-dependent sterile inflammation might play a role in the dental pulp immune defense system. Although this study was limited to in vitro analysis, the findings provide important in- sights into inflammasome activation in aseptic pulpitis. Several studies have suggested mutual regulation between COX- 2 and IL-1b. Th dual COX-2 and soluble epoxide hydrolase (sEH) inhibitor PTUPB inhibit NLRP3 inflammasome activation to exert anti-inflammatory effects in acute lung injury [36]. NLRP3 inflam- masome derived IL-1b secretion in macrophages was reduced by genetic knockdown of COX-2 [37]. Thus, COX-2 targeted treatment can be beneficial for diseases involving NLRP3 inflammasome. However, whether COX-2 regulates the NLRP3 inflammasome and IL-1b production in dental pulp fibroblasts is still unknown. Pharmacological inhibition of COX-2 has been widely imple- mented in experimental studies [38,39]. Since both COX-2 and NLRP3 inflammasome were found to be activated in clustering dental pulp fibroblasts, we applied NS-398, a selective inhibitor of COX-2, to study whether COX-2 can regulate the NLRP3 inflam- masome and IL-1b expression in clustering dental pulp fibroblasts. Our present study found that pharmacological inhibition of COX-2 attenuated activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome and IL-1b expression. This result provides a new understanding that differs from the former traditional relationship in which COX-2 can be stimulated by the cytokine IL-1b in dental pulp cells, gingival fi- broblasts, and periodontal ligament fibroblasts [28,40e43]. Notably, these clustering dental pulp fibroblasts can induce an in- flammatory response without the addition of external cytokines or microbial components [7]. Thus, when dental pulp fibroblasts are stimulated by pathogenic bacteria, whether COX-2 regulates NLRP3 inflammasome-derived IL-1b production is still unknown. Further experiments on COX-2-targeted therapy might be beneficial for regulating cellular biological activity involving the NLRP3 inflam- masome during pulpitis. To date, nemosis has been found to exist in neonatal foreskin, fetal skin, fetal and adult lung fibroblasts, etc [4,6]. It is unclear whether inflammasome activity and IL-1b secretion can be induced in fibroblasts other than DPFs. Further research to evaluate whether inflammasome activity and IL-1b secretion can be induced in other type of fibroblasts is needed, which could ultimately be used more extensively in potential biomedical application. 5. Conclusions To our knowledge, it is the first report to investigate the role of NLRP3 and AIM2 inflammasomes in fibroblasts activation (nemo- sis). The work indicates that the human DPFs can spontaneously express NLRP3 and AIM2 and secrete IL-1b without the addition of external cytokines or microbial components. In addition, pharma- cological inhibition of COX-2, attenuated activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome and IL-1b expression in clustering human DPFs. Thus, nemosis can become a powerful model for studying the mechanisms of the sterile inflammation that occurs during pulpitis. Hopefully, the results of this study will aid in the discovery of new anti-inflammatory treatments for pulpitis.                       Guide

                      You do not type anything

                      Select the words in the Methods text, then press Use highlighted text. The exact words you selected are what gets recorded.

                      Typing is what this design removes. Given a sentence like the one below, one person types RIPA, another types the whole parenthesis, a third types the clause. All three read the paper correctly and disagree in the data. Selecting removes that entirely.

                      The cell total protein was isolated using RIPA (Applygen Technologies Inc., Beijing, China). Protease inhibitor (CWBIO, Shanghai, China) was added into the RIPA at a ratio of 1:100.

                      For lysis buffer there, mark just RIPA, the first time it appears.

                      How much to select

                      The shortest phrase that states the detail. Not the whole sentence when three words will do, and not the vendor, city or catalog number unless the question asks for them.

                      Every question shows a worked example under What to mark. Follow it rather than your instinct.

                      When a paper gives several

                      Many papers list a table of antibodies with eight RRIDs, eight catalog numbers and several vendors. Mark them all. Press Use highlighted text once per value and they stack up in a numbered list. Press Done, next when the list is complete.

                      If they sit in a contiguous table or a run-on list, selecting the whole block in one go is fine and much faster. You are marking where the evidence is, not counting.

                      The same value repeated does not need marking twice. A paper that uses 1:1000 for six antibodies has one dilution, not six.

                      If you stop before you have them all, tick 'There are more of these than I marked'. That box matters more than it looks: without it, someone who stopped at five of eight cannot be told apart from someone who found everything, and the three they skipped would count against the extractor as mistakes it did not make.

                      What counts

                      Judge only what is written. Not what the authors almost certainly did, and not what is standard practice in the field.

                      Judge the text shown. The page shows the Methods section, and sometimes figure legends or the full text where no Methods heading was found. A detail in the text shown counts, legend included. A detail found only in a legend or supplementary file that is not shown is Not stated.

                      Unclear is a real answer. It is set aside rather than counted either way, so use it when you are genuinely stuck rather than guessing.

                      The shaded sentences

                      The faint tint marks the western blot section: sentences that name the blot, the gel, the membrane or its detection. It is the same for every question on a paper. Blot section jumps between its blocks. Other assays are left untinted, but an item can still be stated elsewhere, for example in a figure legend.

                      The warmer marks are the sentences most likely to hold the answer to the open question, and Prev and Next step between them.

                      Both are search aids and nothing more. Fixed keyword rules pick them; they are never told the answer, they sometimes shade irrelevant sentences and sometimes miss the right one. Read around them, and judge only the text.
