What counts as a western blot. Western blot means proteins separated on a gel, transferred to a membrane and detected with an antibody. An OxyBlot counts. A dot blot or slot blot, of RNA or of protein, does not; neither does a capillary system such as Wes or Jess, which has no membrane, or a membrane probed only with streptavidin or a lectin, which uses no antibody. Items marked 'Western blot only' do not count the same thing described for another assay in the paper: qPCR, immunofluorescence, IHC, flow cytometry, ELISA, mass spectrometry, or a gel that was stained rather than blotted. Judge the text the page shows, including any figure legend printed in it; a detail found only in a legend or supplement that is not shown is not stated. A method given only by citation ('as previously described [12]') is not stated: mark only what the text itself says. The Shortcut citation item is the exception: it records the citation phrase itself. Some PDFs drop the micro sign or print it as m ('50 g', '25 mg' for micrograms); rule on the intended unit.

Introduction Glaucoma is a disease characterized by optic nerve atrophy and visual field defects and is the leading cause of irreversible blindness worldwide [1]. Regardless of the type of glaucoma, the most important pathological basis is the persistent death of retinal ganglion cells (RGCs) and their axons, which form the optic nerve [2]. High intraocular pressure (IOP) is the most critical diagnostic and therapeutic target for clinical glaucoma [3, 4]. However, in clinical practice, it has been found that some glaucoma patients still have optic nerve damage even after the IOP has been reduced [5]. Terefore, the optic nerve should be protected while decreasing the IOP. Gynostemma glycoside, also known as seven-leaf gall, is a perennial herbaceous vine climbing plant of the genus Gynostemma (Gynostemma Blume Bijdr) in the family Cucurbitaceae [6]. It was first recorded in Zhu Nan’s “Te Materia Medica” in the Ming Dynasty and is known as “the divine herb,” “the herb of immortality and longevity,” “the second ginseng,” and “the southern ginseng.” Gynostemma glycosides have a positive effect on improving Hindawi Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine Volume 2022, Article ID 9963754, 9 pages https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/9963754 microcirculation, scavenging free radicals, as well as im- proving the nonspecific immunity of the body. It also has anti-inflammatory, antitumor, and antioxidant properties [7, 8]. Gynostemma glycoside has also shown good pro- tective effects in neurological diseases such as Alzheimer’s disease, Parkinson’s disease, depression, and hypoxic brain injury. Gynostemma glycoside protects nerves through anti- inflammation, antioxidative stress, and promoting nerve regeneration [9]. In this study, we investigated the protective effect of gynostemma glycosides in regulating the STAT3/ JAK2 signaling pathway on the optic nerve of rats with chronic high intraocular pressure from the anti-inflam- matory aspect so as to identify a safer and more effective drug treatment for patients with glaucomatous optic neu- ropathy and to further advance research on the use of Chinese medicine for optic nerve protection. 2. Tollefsbol1,2,3,4,5* Page 2 of 20 Rahman and Tollefsbol Cancer Cell International (2024) 24:422 Introduction Breast cancer (BC) is highly prevalent and ranks as the second most common cause of cancer-related deaths in women worldwide [1, 2]. Despite substantial prog­ ress in understanding the mechanisms of BC, success­ ful and effective treatment of BC remains challenging due to the complex and heterogeneous nature of the disease. Conventional BC therapies suffer from limita­ tions including short-term and long-term side effects and therapy resistance that has led to intensive investi­ gation for discovering safe and effective approaches for prevention and treatment of BC [3, 4]. Recent discover­ ies indicate a strong connection of epigenetic anomalies with BC pathogenesis. A growing body of evidence has pointed to a widespread reshuffling in histone acetyla­ tion, histone methylation, and DNA methylation patterns throughout neoplastic transformation and progression of BC [5]. Consequently, targeting epigenetic regulation has emerged as a promising strategy to combat BC. To date, several epigenetics drugs have been approved by the FDA for cancer treatment underscoring the importance of epigenome-modulating compounds in cancer treat­ ment. While studies have documented the epigenetic mechanisms modifying ability of dietary phytochemicals in BC, potential adverse effects of synthetic drugs further places diet-derived phytochemicals as an effective and safe alternative to synthetic drugs [6]. Additionally, BC- related epigenetic mechanisms appear to be more eas­ ily reversible, and plastic compared to cancer-associated genetic abnormalities which renders epigenetic phyto­ chemicals as a promising therapeutic choice [7]. Past investigations from our laboratory have success­ fully developed a novel strategy of combining two or more dietary phytochemicals to target in BC. Such com­ binatorial strategies have been reported to be effective and safe in targeting multiple BC-associated epigenetic anomalies and consequentially impede BC hallmarks [6]. Select combinatorial approaches offer advantages over single-agent therapeutic strategies by better address­ ing tumor heterogeneity, widening therapeutic margin and therapeutic resistance [8]. In this context, our study investigates a novel combination of two dietary phyto­ chemicals, Phenethyl Isothiocyanate (PEITC) and Witha­ ferin A (WA), to target multiple epigenetic pathways and inhibit human BC cells. PEITC is an isothiocyanate available in cruciferous veg­ etables (e.g., broccoli, watercress, cabbage) that can act as a histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor [9, 10]. PEITC has been reported to have profound anticancer activity in a wide variety of cancers including BC [11]. At the molec­ ular level, PEITC has been shown to target several cell signaling pathways crucial for BC survival, growth and progression including mTOR/Akt-, NRF2-, PI3K/Akt- and HIF1α/VEGF- pathways [12]. On the other hand, WA is a steroidal lactone abundant in Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) and known to be a DNA methyl­ transferases (DNMT) inhibitor [13, 14]. WA exhibits inhibitory properties against many cancers including BC in both chemoprevention and therapeutic settings [15]. To date a few molecular targets for WA have been well characterized in BC including p53, FOXO3a, STAT3, ERα, ERK, JAK, IAP and NF-κB [16]. However, epigen­ etic mechanisms targeted by PEITC and WA are less known especially in the context of BC. The HDAC inhibi­ tory effect of PEITC has been confirmed by loci selective hyperacetylation of p21 in addition to reduction of global HDAC activity in prostate cancer. Recent studies have shown that PEITC may block HDAC binding to active chromatin in colon cancer cells [17, 18]. Further, we have reported transcriptional reduction of DNMT1 and DNMT3A in MCF-7 cells following WA treatment [19]. Our study aimed at elucidating the molecular mecha­ nisms of PEITC and WA in modulating epigenetic pathways associated with BC and how PEITC and WA- mediated epigenetic alterations impact BC-associated hallmarks including cellular proliferation, apoptosis and tumorigenicity. We observed that combined PEITC and WA treatment synergistically inhibit cell proliferation, induce apoptosis, and suppress tumorigenicity in BC cells due, at least partially, to combinatorial treatment- induced alteration in DNA methylation and histone acetylation pathway as well as miRNome profiles. Our study also demonstrated that the PEITC + WA treat­ ment-associated expressional alteration of cell cycle and apoptosis-associated proteins closely resembles expres­ sional alteration of cell cycle and apoptosis-associated proteins due to HDAC8 and DNMT3B knockdown. Thus, PEITC + WA combination may target histone acetylation and DNA methylation pathways to inhibit BC-associated hallmarks in MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 cells. Furthermore, a combinatorial phytochemical strategy enables targeting many cellular signaling path­ ways abrogated in BC to target multiple cancer hallmarks. In this regard, PEITC and WA are two important dietary compounds that have potent anticancer properties [12, 15]. Although several molecular mechanisms have been proposed for their anticancer properties, relatively few studies have focused on how PEITC and WA neutralize epigenetic anomalies associated with BC. Therefore, our primary focus was on determining how PEITC and WA combination affects multiple epigenetic processes as well as exploring how PEITC + WA-mediated epigenetic alter­ ation counteracts BC-associated hallmarks including cel­ lular proliferation, apoptosis and tumorigenicity. Accord­ ing to the drug-drug interaction analysis, Chou-Talalay, HSA and ZIP models consistently indicated synergistic interaction between PEITC and WA for multiple combi­ natorial doses in BC cell lines. Despite multiple potential combinations of PEITC and WA, we adopted a compre­ hensive perspective for optimal dose selection. Several other phytochemical combinations have been reported to exhibit selective cytotoxicity against BC cells [6]. A number of mechanisms may contribute to the observed cancer specific cytotoxicity of PEITC and WA combination. Differential expression of drug and xenobiotic metabolizing enzymes between normal and breast cancer tissue is a factor that could influence drug responses. For example, expression profile of several cyto­ chrome P450 enzymes (e.g., CYP2D6, CYP1A1, CYP2B6, etc.) may impact therapeutic effectiveness of tamoxifen by controlling activation and clearance whereas several P450 genes were found to be expressed selectively in BC [47, 48]. In line with the above finding, Spink et al., also documented differential expression of CYP1A1 and CYP1B1 in MCF10A, MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 [49]. Genetic or epigenetic differences between neoplastic and normal cells constitute another important deterministic factor for cancer selective cytotoxicity. Such genetic or epigenetic divergence often manifested as transcriptional alterations that may hypersensitize transformed cells to a compound. For example, HDAC-inhibitor vorinostat has been reported to instigate equivalent histone hyperacety­ lation in normal and transformed cells while selectively killing transformed cells due to the hypersensitivity of transformed cells toward histone hyperacetylation-medi­ ated transcriptional alterations [50, 51]. In this regard, we utilized epigenetic phytochemical PEITC and WA that are capable of neutralizing BC-associated abrogation in histone deacetylation and DNA methylation respectively. Furthermore, this dietary combinatorial regimen is phys­ iologically attainable by consuming ∼ 2.5 cups of Water­ cress and ∼185 mg of Ashwagandha extract powder (6% WA) by an adult per day, respectively [52-54]. PEITC and WA combination impeded the progres­ sion of BC by counteracting BC-associated hallmarks including cell proliferation, apoptosis bypass and tumori­ genesis. Our results indicated that PEITC and WA com­ bination could synergistically induce a cancer specific G2/M-phase arrest in studied BC cells. We observed differential expressional alteration of Cyclin B1/CDK1 and their negative regulators p21 and p27 in MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 cells as a result of combined PEITC and WA treatment. This finding shed light on the molecular mechanisms through which the combination of PEITC and WA induced cell-cycle arrest on BC cells since inhi­ bition of mitotic complex CDK1-cyclin B triggers G2/M transition hindrance and consequential G2/M-phase arrest [55]. Our results from apoptosis assay demon­ strated about 2-2.5-fold increase in apoptosis of studied BC cells due to combinatorial PEITC and WA treatment. Importantly, we observed an upregulation of pro-apop­ totic BAX in MCF-7 and a downregulation of anti- apoptotic BCL-2 expression in MDA-MB-231. Indeed, balance between Bcl-2 and Bax expression act as a regu­ lator that dictates whether a cell will survive or undergo apoptosis in response to an apoptotic stimulus by altering apoptotic threshold for human cancer cells [56]. Combi­ natorial treatment-induced BAX or BCL-2 expressional change, therefore, mechanistically decreased cellular resistance to apoptotic stimuli by lowering apoptotic threshold. However, BAX: BCL-2 ratio may not neces­ sarily explain the magnitude of apoptosis observed with single compound intervention in our study which intro­ duces the possibilities of involvement of other potential molecular players behind PEITC or WA-induced cell death. In a recent investigation with WA and sulfora­ phane, we have observed that single as well as combina­ torial treatments induce the expression of PUMA [57]. This BH3-only protein may further activate Bax/Bak in order to induce cell death through mitochondria dys­ function [58]. On the other hand, mammosphere assay results revealed a significant reduction in TFE efficiency in both MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 breast cancer cells following combinatorial PEITC + WA treatment. We, therefore, focused on assessing the impact of com­ binatorial treatments on the stem cell compartment of MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 cells population. Specifically, we focused on the CD44+/CD24- stem cell population, which is associated with breast cancer stem cells (CSCs). We found that the PEITC and WA combination was the only treatment that significantly reduced the CD44+/ CD24- stem cell population in both cell lines. This is a critical finding as CSCs are believed to play a key role in tumor initiation and recurrence while serving as an indi­ cator for tumorigenicity [60]. Further studies indicated that PEITC and WA com­ bination has a profound impact on the expression of key epigenetic regulators. Both in MCF-7 and MDA- MB-231 cells, multiple epigenetic regulators involved in histone deacetylation and DNA methylation processes were downregulated following PEITC and WA combina­ tion treatment. In parallel with protein expression data, HDAC and DNMT activity assessment upon combina­ torial treatment showed significantly decreased global HDAC and DNMT activity in both MCF-7 and MDA- MB-231 cells. The expressional downregulation simulta­ neously with a decreased HDAC and DNMT enzymatic activity suggest that the combination treatment plays a crucial role in epigenetic modifications that may con­ tribute to the observed phenotypic outcomes upon com­ bination treatment in BC cells such as cell cycle arrest, apoptosis induction and tumorigenic potential reduction. We proposed that the epigenetic impact of PEITC and WA combination converge to the molecular level in order to manifest phenotypic outcome. Thus, we were interested to investigate if we could alter the expres­ sion of cell cycle- and apoptosis-associated proteins that are influenced by PEITC + WA treatment through his­ tone acetylation and DNA-methylation pathway-related machinery knockdown in MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 cells. We selected HDAC8 and DNMT3B as targets to interfere with histone acetylation and DNA methylation pathway, respectively, since the expression of both pro­ teins were downregulated by PEITC + WA treatment. Interestingly, HDAC8 and DNMT3B knockdown reca­ pitulated the molecular signatures of PEITC + WA treat­ ment by altering the expression of multiple cell cycle and apoptosis-associated protein expression including p21, p27, Cyclin B1 and BAX. This finding further strength­ ened our assumption that PEITC + WA treatment targets histone acetylation and DNA methylation pathways to exert anti-breast cancer effects. In this regard, expression of p21 and p27 have been reported to be dysregulated by DNA methylation and histone deacetylation in multiple hematologic and solid cancers, including BC [61, 62]. Most strikingly, p21 could undergo p53-independent epi­ genetic repression in T-cell acute lymphoblastic leuke­ mia which was reversed by combinatorial vorinostat and decitabine treatment [63]. Furthermore, apoptosis regu­ lator BAX and BCL-2 could undergo hypermethylation- mediated silencing in neoplastic situations [64]. On the other hand, epigenetic regulation including DNA meth­ ylation and histone modification play a critical role in the acquisition and maintenance of cancer stemness that contribute to tumor growth, recurrence, and treatment resistance [65, 66]. Increasing evidence suggests a close association between BC stemness and miRNA in maintenance and regulation of CSCs function [67] which led us to inves­ tigate the effect of combined PEITC and WA treatment on miRNome profiles of MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 cells. Differential miRNA expression analysis revealed 30 differentially expressed miRNAs in MCF-7 cells, with 12 upregulated and 18 downregulated miRNAs. Similarly, in MDA-MB-231 cells, 23 miRNAs showed differential expressions, with 14 upregulated and 9 downregulated. Assigning biological themes on the dif­ ferentially expressed miRNAs further highlighted the fact that differentially expressed miRNAs may control CSC behavior through the regulation of cellular mor­ phogenesis and cellular homeostasis. The differentially expressed miRNAs in MCF-7 cells have shown func­ tional implications in inhibiting tumorigenesis, cell pro­ liferation, invasion, migration, and stemness in other cancer types. Similarly, miRNAs that are differentially expressed in MDA-MB-231 cells have been reported to be involved in cell proliferation, invasion, and metastasis across various cancer types. Moreover, the expressional upregulation of hsa-mir-1246 or the expressional down­ regulation of hsa-mir-4454 in MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 BC cells convincingly correlates with the cell viability decrease upon combinatorial PEITC and WA treatment. We further noted that differentially expressed miRNAs are associated with histone modification, transcriptional regulation, and chromatin condensation. We, therefore, assumed a multidimensional interplay between BC stem­ ness and combinatorial treatment-induced changes in miRNA networks as well as epigenetic regulation. Throughout the investigation, we have observed syner­ gistic interaction between PEITC and WA in neutraliz­ ing BC-associated hallmarks both at the phenotypic and molecular level which indicates that combined action of PEITC and WA is more effective than either compound alone. Indeed, combinatorial therapeutic strategy helps to improve therapeutic margin and mitigate toxicities associated with anticancer drugs. Additionally, combina­ torial therapeutics could reduce the chance of treatment resistance by better addressing tumor heterogeneity [68]. Such balance in mechanisms of action may contribute to observed synergism in PEITC and WA effect. Accumulating evidence suggests DNA methyla­ tion could influence the chromatin orientation by influ­ encing histone modifications and vice versa [69]. DNMTs have been reported to physically interact with HDACs and can be co-recruited to transcriptional repressor com­ plex (e.g., MeCP2) for transcriptional repression [70]. Several epigenetically active phytochemicals have been studied in clinical settings against BC. Some of the phyto­ chemicals have profound DNMT inhibitory effects such as EGCG and Polyphenon E. These clinical studies may serve as the foundation for the combinatorial application of PEITC and WA against BC in clinical settings against BC which warrants further clinical investigation. We are presenting, for the first time, findings on the epigenetic impacts of combined PEITC and WA in any form of cancer in this study. This study also provided compelling evidence that combined PEITC and WA has synergistic anticancer effects against hormonal-depen­ dent and -independent MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 BC cells. Our results indicate that combined PEITC and WA treatment can modulate BC-associated aberration in multiple epigenetic pathways in MCF-7 and MDA- MB-231 BC cells that lead to reduced cell viability, reduced cell proliferation, decreased tumorigenicity and enhanced apoptosis. Moreover, our miRNA expression profiling alteration due to combined PEITC and WA treatment suggests a potential role for miRNAs in medi­ ating the anti-BC effect of this combination in MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 BC cells. These findings also offer promising insights for development of more effective combinatorial therapeutic approaches for BC treatment by targeting epigenetic mechanisms. Conclusion Our study provides for the first time strong evidence that PEITC and WA combination can hinder BC syn­ ergistically at optimal concentrations that are safe and physiologically relevant. We show that PEITC and WA combination can hinder BC by interfering with cell cycle and apoptosis pathways, reducing tumorigenicity, and altering miRNA expression profiles of MCF-7 and MDA- MB-231 BC cells. In addition, we have presented find­ ings on how PEITC + WA influence several key epigenetic mechanisms. All these findings warrant further investiga­ tion with other breast cancer cell lines to attain a com­ prehensive understating of how combined PEITC and WA treatment interferes with BC-associated epigenetic anomalies. Our findings open new avenues for further research and the development of novel therapeutic strat­ egies for BC treatment. Further studies, including in vivo experiments and clinical trials, will be needed to assess the safety and efficacy of this combination treatment in clinical settings. Synthesis of liposomes and liposome-ICG Liposomes were synthesized by using a thin film hydration method,17 and then extruded. Briefly, DPPC, DSPE-PEG, and cholesterol lipids in a 1.5:1.5:1 (wt. ratio) were mixed in chloroform and then a lipid film was formed by the evaporation of chloroform. Next, the lipid film was hydrated for 30 minutes at 60°C by the addition of PBS solution, which produced heterogeneous and multivesicular liposomes. The obtained solution was passed through a 200 nm polycarbonate filter and fixed by using an Avanti Mini-Extruder (Avanti Polar Lipids) to obtain liposomes. ICG was loaded into liposomes during the hydration process. An aqueous solution containing ICG was added to liposomes at weight ratio of 1:100 and hydrated for 30 minutes at 60°C, then extruded through a 200 nm polycarbonate filter mounted in an Avanti Mini-Extruder. Excess ICG solution was removed by using 100 k Da centrifugal filters (Merck Millipore Co., Dublin, Ireland). Discussion Earlier, our team preliminarily demonstrated that VB significantly promoted the elimination of Aβ plaques and NFTs [23]; however, the exact mechanism by which VB exerts neuroprotection in AD remains unclear. In this study, we explored the anti-inflammatory role of VB in APP/PS1 mice based on proteomics analysis. VB prevented microglia and astrocytes against activation and restrained the expression levels of iNOS, NO, and other pro-inflammatory cytokines and the activation of NF-κB-p65 signaling-related proteins. These results were further confirmed in LPS-induced BV2 and Aβ1-42-stimulated N2a cells. Therefore, VB plays a neuroprotective role in AD, as it inhibits neuroinflammation by blocking the NF-κB-p65 pathway. Through positron emission tomography, researchers have evidenced neuroinflamma- tion takes place in the early stage of AD [37,38]. GV-971 has been confirmed to reverse cognition dysfunction in mild-to-moderate AD by regulating neuroinflammation [18]. Therefore, neuroinflammation is a vital aspect to focus on during the prevention and treat- ment of AD. In this study, based on the results of proteomic analysis, the expressions of PLXNB2 and SORBS2 were significantly discrepant among all experimental groups, which are closely associated with inflammation. PLXNB2 could promote inflammatory response both in allergic airway inflammation and psoriasis [39,40]. In the CNS, PLXNB2 released by astrocytes caused inflammation in multiple sclerosis and experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis by connecting with CD100 (the ligand of PLXNB2) in microglia [41]. Additionally, the downregulated SORBS2 was observed in the heart tissues of LPS-treated C57BL/6 mice [42]. The silence of SORBS2 in mouse embryonic fibroblasts led to the higher expression of IL-6 RNA [27]. Accordingly, VB regulated the levels of PLXNB2 and SORBS2 Nutrients 2022, 14, 1417 12 of 16 in brains of APP/PS1 mice, LPS-induced BV2 cells, and Aβ1-42-exposed N2a cells, which implied the neuroprotection of VB was related to the regulation of inflammation. Neuroinflammation is characterized by the activation of astrocytes and microglia. There are two extreme types of activated microglia: the pro-inflammatory M1 subtype (classically activated) and the anti-inflammatory M2 subtype (alternatively activated) [43]. In our study, VB lessened the production of M1 markers including iNOS, IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α [44]. This observation suggests that VB mitigates the M1 polarization of microglia. Furthermore, VB upregulated the levels of anti-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-4, IL-10 as well as TGF-β in the APP/PS1 mice. Interestingly, in the CNS, TGF-β discharged by mesenchymal stromal cells played an critical role in transforming the M1 microglia to M2 subtype and attenuating NF-κB signaling [45]. TGF-β produced by phagocytizing microglia could block the production of autocrine TNF-α and repress oxidative stress, thus supporting the survival of phagocytizing microglia [46]. These studies imply that TGF-β could regulate the function of microglia towards that of M2 subtype, thus dissolving neuroinflammation. Consequently, VB regulates the polarization of microglia. During the early onset of AD, scavenger receptors (SRs) are abundant on the mem- branes of activated microglia and are involved in the engulfment of Aβ deposits [47]. However, persistent stimulation caused by Aβ plaques leads to chronic neuroinflammation and increased release of pro-inflammatory factors, such as TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 from the activated microglia, which in turn inhibits the expression levels of SRs. This promotes the aggregation of Aβ plaques that is followed by exacerbation of neuroinflammation and AD pathology [43,48,49]. Pro-inflammatory factors secreted by microglia activate astrocytes to produce more pro-inflammatory mediators, causing an amplification of neuroinflam- mation [50]. Consistently, the reactive microglia and astrocytes in the brains of APP/PS1 mice were significantly repressed by VB, thus confirming that VB exerts neuroprotection via neuroinflammation prevention. Apart from extracellular Aβ plaques and intracellular NFTs, deficiency in neurons is also noted in patients with AD [51]. BDNF is responsible for neuronal survival and synaptic plasticity, which helps in alleviating cognitive impairment [52]. A higher level of BDNF was observed in the APP/PS1 mice after VB administration, confirming its anti-AD properties. Glia-neuron interactions are crucial for AD pathology. The pro-inflammatory cytokines released by microglia not only cause direct toxicity to neurons but also aggregate the formation of Aβ plaques, which forms a vicious cycle during AD pathology associated with neuroinflammation [38]. Our cellular co-culture experiment was consistent with this conclusion. In Aβ1-42-stimulated N2a cells, VB effectively dissolved inflammatory response as evidenced by the lower levels of pro-inflammatory mediators and higher expression of anti-inflammatory ones. The activation of microglia and astrocytes has been reported to be closely linked with Aβ plaque formation and tau pathology, thereby leading to the occurrence of AD-like symptoms such as memory decline and learning disorders [53]. Nevertheless, in our previous study, VB improved AD-related symptoms in APP/PS1 mice [23]. These results confirm the important role of the anti-neuroinflammatory effect of VB in AD. According to previous studies, NF-κB, a member of the transcription factor Rel family, regulates neuroinflammation [54]. Once stimuli such as Aβ plaques and LPS are recognized by microglia, IKKα+β, an inhibitor of κB kinase, is phosphorylated. This is followed by the phosphorylation of IκBα at serine 32 and 36 positions [14,55]. IκBα originally functions to prevent NF-κB from translocation towards nucleus. After the phosphorylated IκBα dissociating from p50/p65, the NF-κB heterodimer successfully translocate towards nucleus for further transcription of pro-inflammatory factors [14]. Similar changes were observed in VB-treated APP/PS1 mice, LPS-exposed BV2 cells, and Aβ1-42-stimulated N2a cells. The anti-neuroinflammatory effect of VB suppresses the activation of NF-κB-p65 signaling. We noted that this study had some limitations. Since there are many regulators that are implicated in NF-κB signaling [56], the effect of VB exposure on upstream regulators of NF-κB signaling needs to be explored further, both in glial cells and neurons. Additionally, Nutrients 2022, 14, 1417 13 of 16 researchers have evidenced that PLXNB2-CD100 engaged in the regulation of neuroinflam- mation via the microglia-astrocyte communication [41]. Our study has also verified the essential role of PLXNB2 in the VB-mediated amelioration of neuroinflammation. It may be interesting to further explore whether VB engages in the regulation of microglia-astrocyte communication and whether PLXNB2-CD100 is also involved during this process. 5. Conclusions Contrasted with APP/PS1 mice, VB significantly prevented microglia and astrocyte against activation by regulating the NF-κB-p65 pathway. VB suppressed the production of pro-inflammatory mediators and upregulated those of anti-inflammatory ones by blocking NF-κB-p65 from translocating towards nucleus in LPS-treated BV2 cells. VB protects N2a cells against stimulation of conditional medium from Aβ1-42-treated BV2 cells and also plays a neuroprotective role by inhibiting neuroinflammation in Aβ1-42-exposed N2a cells. Consequently, VB-mediated anti-AD effects are at least partly related to its anti- neuroinflammatory properties, which contributes to its neuroprotective mechanism and provided more experimental evidence for its application as a promising treatment for AD.                       Guide

                      You do not type anything

                      Select the words in the Methods text, then press Use highlighted text. The exact words you selected are what gets recorded.

                      Typing is what this design removes. Given a sentence like the one below, one person types RIPA, another types the whole parenthesis, a third types the clause. All three read the paper correctly and disagree in the data. Selecting removes that entirely.

                      The cell total protein was isolated using RIPA (Applygen Technologies Inc., Beijing, China). Protease inhibitor (CWBIO, Shanghai, China) was added into the RIPA at a ratio of 1:100.

                      For lysis buffer there, mark just RIPA, the first time it appears.

                      How much to select

                      The shortest phrase that states the detail. Not the whole sentence when three words will do, and not the vendor, city or catalog number unless the question asks for them.

                      Every question shows a worked example under What to mark. Follow it rather than your instinct.

                      When a paper gives several

                      Many papers list a table of antibodies with eight RRIDs, eight catalog numbers and several vendors. Mark them all. Press Use highlighted text once per value and they stack up in a numbered list. Press Done, next when the list is complete.

                      If they sit in a contiguous table or a run-on list, selecting the whole block in one go is fine and much faster. You are marking where the evidence is, not counting.

                      The same value repeated does not need marking twice. A paper that uses 1:1000 for six antibodies has one dilution, not six.

                      If you stop before you have them all, tick 'There are more of these than I marked'. That box matters more than it looks: without it, someone who stopped at five of eight cannot be told apart from someone who found everything, and the three they skipped would count against the extractor as mistakes it did not make.

                      What counts

                      Judge only what is written. Not what the authors almost certainly did, and not what is standard practice in the field.

                      Judge the text shown. The page shows the Methods section, and sometimes figure legends or the full text where no Methods heading was found. A detail in the text shown counts, legend included. A detail found only in a legend or supplementary file that is not shown is Not stated.

                      Unclear is a real answer. It is set aside rather than counted either way, so use it when you are genuinely stuck rather than guessing.

                      The shaded sentences

                      The faint tint marks the western blot section: sentences that name the blot, the gel, the membrane or its detection. It is the same for every question on a paper. Blot section jumps between its blocks. Other assays are left untinted, but an item can still be stated elsewhere, for example in a figure legend.

                      The warmer marks are the sentences most likely to hold the answer to the open question, and Prev and Next step between them.

                      Both are search aids and nothing more. Fixed keyword rules pick them; they are never told the answer, they sometimes shade irrelevant sentences and sometimes miss the right one. Read around them, and judge only the text.
