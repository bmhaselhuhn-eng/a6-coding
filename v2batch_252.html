What counts as a western blot. Western blot means proteins separated on a gel, transferred to a membrane and detected with an antibody. An OxyBlot counts. A dot blot or slot blot, of RNA or of protein, does not; neither does a capillary system such as Wes or Jess, which has no membrane, or a membrane probed only with streptavidin or a lectin, which uses no antibody. Items marked 'Western blot only' do not count the same thing described for another assay in the paper: qPCR, immunofluorescence, IHC, flow cytometry, ELISA, mass spectrometry, or a gel that was stained rather than blotted. Judge the text the page shows, including any figure legend printed in it; a detail found only in a legend or supplement that is not shown is not stated. A method given only by citation ('as previously described [12]') is not stated: mark only what the text itself says. The Shortcut citation item is the exception: it records the citation phrase itself. Some PDFs drop the micro sign or print it as m ('50 g', '25 mg' for micrograms); rule on the intended unit.

Every question carries its own What to mark rule with a worked example. Follow it rather than your instinct: consistency between reviewers matters more here than any single choice. Full rules under Guide.

Total protein stain
A total-protein stain used to normalize or check the blot: Ponceau S or REVERT on the membrane, stain-free imaging used for normalization, or Coomassie stated as the blot's loading or transfer check, including a parallel gel stained and stated as the blot's loading check. A stained gel with no stated link to a blot, or a stain-free gel named only as a product, does not count. Western blot only.
What to mark. Mark the stain name only.In confirmed by Ponceau S staining mark Ponceau S. Nothing to mark in run on a 4-15% TGX Stain-Free gel with no normalization stated, in any stain on a purity, expression, preparative, mass-spec or antibody-QC gel, or in a stain on a gel that was never blotted and is not stated as the blot's loading check.

Materials Fresh fruiting bodies of L. edodes species of commercial ori- gin were used for the experiments and for establishing in vitro cultures. The fragments were then sterilized with 15% sodium hypochlorite (NaClO) solution for several minutes. After rinsing with redistilled water, sterile fragments of fruiting bodies were transferred under laminar flow to Oddoux medium solidified with agar [15]. To obtain a large amount of biomass, it is necessary that the biomass grown on in vitro cultures on solid medium be transferred to a modified Oddoux liquid substrate. Therefore, biomass obtained from in vitro cultures on solid medium was transferred to Erlenmeyer flasks containing 250 mL of liquid medium; the initial mass of the inoculum was 0.1 g. The in vitro cultures were allowed to grow for 21 days on Oddoux medium at 25 ± 2 °C, under a photoperiod of 16 h light (900 lx) and 8 h dark, on an ALTEL shaker (Poland) at 140 rpm. Experimental In Vitro Cultures of L. edodes The following materials were prepared for the planned exper- iments: biomass of L. edodes (control) was obtained on a liquid Oddoux medium and on the same liquid medium with the addition of zinc compounds, such as zinc hydrogen aspar- tate (C8H12N2O8Zn) at a concentration of 200 mg/L and zinc sulfate (ZnSO4) at a concentration of 174.47 mg/L, and copper compounds, such as copper gluconate (C12H22CuO14) at a concentration of 72.88 mg/L and copper sulfate (CuSO4) at a concentration of 33.60 mg/L. After culturing, mycelium was separated from the medium, frozen, and lyophilized (Freezone 4.5 lyophilizator, Labonco; temperature -40 °C) for further ex- periments as in previous study [16, 17]. Analysis of Bioelements in L. edodes Mycelium The amount of elements in the fruiting bodies and mycelium of L. edodes and extracts obtained by digestion with artificial 604 Muszyńska et al. digestive juices was determined using TXRF method. The TXRF method was chosen for this study due to several sig- nificant aspects, such as high sensitivity, precision, accuracy, and repeatability of analyses, as well as possibility to achieve faster measurement with a very large number of samples. The optimization of mineralization conditions for analysis of sam- ples in combination with the TXRF method enabled optimal analysis of bioelements in the fruiting bodies, mycelial cul- tures, and extracts obtained from L. edodes. For this analysis, 0.2 g samples of lyophilized mushroom material were weighed, with an accuracy of 0.01 g, and were transferred to Teflon vessels, to which 2 mL of H2O2 solution (30%) and 4 mL of concentrated HNO3 solution (65%) were added. Mineralization was carried out in Magnum II micro- wave apparatus (ERTEC) in three stages of 10 min each, at a power of 70% and 100%, respectively, maintaining the tem- perature of the device at 290 °C. After mineralization, the solutions were transferred to quartz evaporators and evaporat- ed on a heating plate at 150 °C to remove excess reagents and water. The residue obtained after evaporation was quantita- tively transferred to 10-mL volumetric flasks with four-time distilled water. To analyze the composition of bioelements such as K, Ca, Mn, Fe, Cu, Zn, and Se in the prepared test samples, 1000 ppm gallium was used as an internal standard. The composition of elements was measured using a TXRF spectrometer Nanohunter II (Rigaku) equipped with an X- ray tube containing a molybdenum anode at 50 kV for 1000 s. Artificial Gastric Juice A total of 3.2 g of pepsin and 2.0 g of NaCl were dissolved in four-time distilled water, and then to this solution, 80 mL of 1 mol/L HCl was added. The volume of the solution was finally made up to 1000 mL with water [19]. Artificial Intestinal Juice A total of 120 mg bile acids, 20 mg pancreatic extract, and 8.4 g NaHCO3 were dissolved in four- time distilled water, and the volume of the solution was made up to 1000 mL with water [20]. Extracts were obtained from lyophilized biomass from fruiting bodies and in vitro cultures of L. edodes and were digested in vitro using artificial digestive juices in the Gastroel-2014 apparatus as follows [13]. The extraction of mushroom material was carried out under conditions imitating those prevailing in the human body (temperature 37 °C and mixing movements imitating peristaltic movements). About 0.3 g of mushroom material was transferred to 100-mL Erlenmeyer flasks and moistened with 3 mL of artificial saliva solution for 1 min. Then, 20 mL of gastric juice was added to the flasks. The flasks were sealed and placed in the Gastroel- 2014 apparatus for the digestion process. The process was conducted for 30 and 60 min, respectively. The obtained ex- tracts were filtered using of paper filters and syringe mem- brane filters. The remaining mushroom material was again transferred to Erlenmeyer flasks, and 20 mL of intestinal juice was added. Digestion was allowed to proceed for 150 min. After digestion, the extracts were filtered to obtain a clear solution. Each sample was subjected to three independent rep- etitions. The content of elements was also determined by TXRF method described above. Mycelium was extracted five times with 100 mL of methanol using ultrasound at a frequency of 49 kHz for 30 min (Sonic-2, Polsonic, Poland). Following extraction, the methanol in the extracts was evaporated to dryness. Then, 250 mg of dry extract was dissolved in 96% ethanol, filtered through sterile bacteriological syringe filters with a pore diameter of 0.2 μm, and transferred quantitatively to 5-mL volumetric flasks. Finally, the extracts were stored at 4 °C until they were used for analysis of anti-inflammatory activity in RAW 264.7 cell lines. In order to determine the actual bioavailability of Cu, Zn, and Se to the human body, the release of these elements from the mushroom material into artificial digestive juices was in- vestigated.                       The shortest phrase that states the detail. Not the whole sentence when three words will do, and not the vendor, city or catalog number unless the question asks for them.

                      The faint tint marks the western blot section: sentences that name the blot, the gel, the membrane or its detection. It is the same for every question on a paper. Blot section jumps between its blocks. Other assays are left untinted, but an item can still be stated elsewhere, for example in a figure legend.

                      The warmer marks are the sentences most likely to hold the answer to the open question, and Prev and Next step between them.

                      Both are search aids and nothing more. Fixed keyword rules pick them; they are never told the answer, they sometimes shade irrelevant sentences and sometimes miss the right one. Read around them, and judge only the text.

                      Every question, in one place

                      Total protein stainA total-protein stain used to normalize or check the blot: Ponceau S or REVERT on the membrane, stain-free imaging used for normalization, or Coomassie stated as the blot's loading or transfer check, including a parallel gel stained and stated as the blot's loading check. A stained gel with no stated link to a blot, or a stain-free gel named only as a product, does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the stain name only. In confirmed by Ponceau S staining mark Ponceau S. Nothing to mark in run on a 4-15% TGX Stain-Free gel with no normalization stated, in any stain on a purity, expression, preparative, mass-spec or antibody-QC gel, or in a stain on a gel that was never blotted and is not stated as the blot's loading check.
                      Transfer conditionsThe voltage or current, the time, or the temperature of the transfer. Any one counts. The transfer method alone ('wet transfer') does not. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the conditions. In transferred at 100 V for 1 h at 4 °C mark the whole phrase.
                      Exposure timeAn exposure or acquisition time for the blot's signal, on film or on an imager. 'Auto-exposure', 'different exposure times', 'exposed to film' with no time, or a substrate incubation time ('incubated with ECL for 1 min') does not count, and neither does an antibody or cytokine array, which is not a blot. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the time and its unit. In bands were imaged with a 30 s exposure on a ChemiDoc mark 30 s. Nothing to mark in incubated with ECL for 1 min or cells were exposed to 80 uM drug for 30 min.
                      Lysate storage statedHow the blot's lysates or protein extracts were stored before loading: a storage temperature, with or without a duration ('lysates were stored at -80 °C until use'). Storage of tissue, cells, blood or other samples before lysis, and storage of antibodies or reagents, does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the words that state the storage. In the supernatant was aliquoted and stored at -80 °C mark stored at -80 °C. Nothing to mark in tissues were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C.
                      Antibody cloneA clone name or number for a monoclonal antibody. A catalog number, or a vendor's epitope code for a polyclonal (Santa Cruz C-20), does not count. Counts for an antibody used in any assay, not only the blot.
                      Mark: Mark every distinct clone id. In anti-beta-actin (clone AC-15, Sigma) mark AC-15.
                      Membrane source or gradeThe supplier, product name or grade of the blot membrane, such as Immobilon-P (Millipore) or Amersham Hybond. Membrane chemistry or pore size alone does not count, and neither does the supplier of a transfer system or kit that does not name the membrane, or an Immobilon ECL substrate, which is the ECL reagent. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the supplier or product words for the membrane. In transferred to PVDF membranes (Immobilon-P, Millipore) mark Immobilon-P, Millipore. Nothing to mark in transferred to PVDF membranes.
                      Antibody isotypeThe immunoglobulin class or subclass stated for an antibody itself, primary or secondary (IgG, IgG1, IgG2a, IgM, IgY), as in 'rabbit polyclonal IgG' or 'mouse monoclonal IgG2a'. An isotype that names what a secondary recognizes ('goat anti-rabbit IgG'), an isotype control, a normal or control IgG, or a serum antibody response does not count. Host species and clonality belong to Primary antibody host or type. Counts for an antibody used in any assay, not only the blot.
                      Mark: Mark each distinct isotype once. In mouse monoclonal IgG2a anti-GFAP mark IgG2a; in rabbit polyclonal IgG anti-Kif5 mark IgG. Nothing to mark in HRP-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG.
                      Replicate definitionWhat a replicate of the blot was, with the unit named: independent cultures or passages, animals, patients, separate blots, or technical repeats of one lysate. 'Independent experiments' or 'biological replicates' with no unit named does not count, and neither does a count alone. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the words that say what was replicated. In three independent cell cultures, each blotted once mark the whole phrase. Nothing to mark in representative of three independent experiments.
                      Replicate countA number of replicates stated for the western blot, in the blot's paragraph or a blot figure legend. A group size stated for the blot counts ('six rats from each group' in the Western blot paragraph). A legend-wide count applies to a blot panel only when that panel is quantified. A statistics paragraph that names no assay, or a group size stated elsewhere, does not count. You are not being asked whether they are biological or technical. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the number together with the words that make it a replicate count. In western blots are representative of three independent experiments mark three independent experiments. Nothing to mark in qPCR reactions were run in triplicate or in data are mean ± SD of three independent experiments in a statistics paragraph that names no assay.
