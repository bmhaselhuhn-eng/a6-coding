What counts as a western blot. Western blot means proteins separated on a gel, transferred to a membrane and detected with an antibody. An OxyBlot counts. A dot blot or slot blot, of RNA or of protein, does not; neither does a capillary system such as Wes or Jess, which has no membrane, or a membrane probed only with streptavidin or a lectin, which uses no antibody. Items marked 'Western blot only' do not count the same thing described for another assay in the paper: qPCR, immunofluorescence, IHC, flow cytometry, ELISA, mass spectrometry, or a gel that was stained rather than blotted. Judge the text the page shows, including any figure legend printed in it; a detail found only in a legend or supplement that is not shown is not stated. A method given only by citation ('as previously described [12]') is not stated: mark only what the text itself says. The Shortcut citation item is the exception: it records the citation phrase itself. Some PDFs drop the micro sign or print it as m ('50 g', '25 mg' for micrograms); rule on the intended unit.

This applies to the case of PV. PV ethanol extract has been demonstrated to possess inhibitory effects on cervical and colon tumor growth in mice (Chen et al., 2019; Li et al., 2023). However, when PV was prescribed for colon cancer patients, they would take the aqueous decoction (i.e., water extract) orally (McCulloch et al., 2016). Hence, we focused on investigation of the water extract of PV and its active compounds in colon cancer. Herein, we first compared the inhibitory activities of cells viability between PVW and PVE in HCT116 cells using MTT assay. The MTT assay is a popular and versatile method for assessing cells viability, involving the conversion of MTT dye to an insoluble formazan by mitochondrial reductase (Kumar et al., 2018). Results showed that the cell viabilities of HCT116 cells were reduced after treating with PVW and PVE for 48 h.                       Select the words in the Methods text, then press Use highlighted text. The exact words you selected are what gets recorded.

                      Typing is what this design removes. Given a sentence like the one below, one person types RIPA, another types the whole parenthesis, a third types the clause. All three read the paper correctly and disagree in the data. Selecting removes that entirely.

                      The cell total protein was isolated using RIPA (Applygen Technologies Inc., Beijing, China). Protease inhibitor (CWBIO, Shanghai, China) was added into the RIPA at a ratio of 1:100.

                      For lysis buffer there, mark just RIPA, the first time it appears.

                      How much to select

                      The shortest phrase that states the detail. Not the whole sentence when three words will do, and not the vendor, city or catalog number unless the question asks for them.

                      Every question shows a worked example under What to mark. Follow it rather than your instinct.

                      When a paper gives several

                      Many papers list a table of antibodies with eight RRIDs, eight catalog numbers and several vendors. Mark them all. Press Use highlighted text once per value and they stack up in a numbered list. Press Done, next when the list is complete.

                      If they sit in a contiguous table or a run-on list, selecting the whole block in one go is fine and much faster. You are marking where the evidence is, not counting.

                      The same value repeated does not need marking twice. A paper that uses 1:1000 for six antibodies has one dilution, not six.

                      If you stop before you have them all, tick 'There are more of these than I marked'. That box matters more than it looks: without it, someone who stopped at five of eight cannot be told apart from someone who found everything, and the three they skipped would count against the extractor as mistakes it did not make.

                      What counts

                      Judge only what is written. Not what the authors almost certainly did, and not what is standard practice in the field.

                      Judge the text shown. The page shows the Methods section, and sometimes figure legends or the full text where no Methods heading was found. A detail in the text shown counts, legend included. A detail found only in a legend or supplementary file that is not shown is Not stated.

                      Unclear is a real answer. It is set aside rather than counted either way, so use it when you are genuinely stuck rather than guessing.

                      The shaded sentences

                      The faint tint marks the western blot section: sentences that name the blot, the gel, the membrane or its detection. It is the same for every question on a paper. Blot section jumps between its blocks. Other assays are left untinted, but an item can still be stated elsewhere, for example in a figure legend.

                      The warmer marks are the sentences most likely to hold the answer to the open question, and Prev and Next step between them.

                      Both are search aids and nothing more. Fixed keyword rules pick them; they are never told the answer, they sometimes shade irrelevant sentences and sometimes miss the right one. Read around them, and judge only the text.

                      Getting through a batch

                      The Methods text stays pinned on the left while questions scroll on the right. Answers collapse to one line and the next question opens automatically. Click any collapsed line to change it.

                      Your work is saved in this browser as you go, so closing the tab loses nothing. Submit is at the top of the page and turns on once every question is answered and your initials are filled in.

                      Every question, in one place

                      Exposure timeAn exposure or acquisition time for the blot's signal, on film or on an imager. 'Auto-exposure', 'different exposure times', 'exposed to film' with no time, or a substrate incubation time ('incubated with ECL for 1 min') does not count, and neither does an antibody or cytokine array, which is not a blot. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the time and its unit. In bands were imaged with a 30 s exposure on a ChemiDoc mark 30 s. Nothing to mark in incubated with ECL for 1 min or cells were exposed to 80 uM drug for 30 min.
                      Stripping verifiedA statement that stripping was checked as complete before reprobing, for example by re-imaging the stripped membrane with substrate or incubating it with secondary antibody alone. That the membrane was stripped, or the stripping method, does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the statement. In removal of antibody was confirmed by re-exposing the stripped membrane to ECL mark the whole phrase. Nothing to mark in membranes were stripped and reprobed.
                      Protein loaded per laneA mass of protein loaded on the blot's gel, per lane, well or sample ('Proteins (40 ug) were run on SDS gels' counts). A volume on its own does not count, and neither does a mass used for another assay (an IP or pull-down input, a digest, drug or RNA amounts). Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the mass and its unit. In 30 ug of protein was loaded per lane mark 30 ug.
                      Lysis buffer compositionThe lysis buffer's recipe, or the product used. A recipe with at least one component of the base buffer and its concentration counts, and so does a product identified by brand name, vendor or catalog number. A generic name alone, such as 'RIPA buffer', does not count, because RIPA recipes differ, and neither do protease or phosphatase inhibitors added to it, which are their own item. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the recipe, or the product with what identifies it. In RIPA (50 mM Tris pH 7.4, 150 mM NaCl, 1% NP-40, 0.5% deoxycholate, 0.1% SDS) mark the parenthesis; in RIPA (Applygen Technologies) mark the whole phrase. Nothing to mark in lysed in RIPA buffer.
                      Transfer conditionsThe voltage or current, the time, or the temperature of the transfer. Any one counts. The transfer method alone ('wet transfer') does not. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the conditions. In transferred at 100 V for 1 h at 4 °C mark the whole phrase.
                      Antibody diluentWhat an antibody was diluted in for the blot: a named diluent, a plain buffer such as PBS-T, or a recipe such as 5% BSA in TBST. 'In blocking buffer' counts when the blocking buffer's composition is stated. A diluent for IHC, IF or flow cytometry does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the diluent words, not the dilution. In primary antibodies were diluted 1:1000 in 5% BSA/TBST mark 5% BSA/TBST. Nothing to mark in an IHC antibody diluted in PBS.
                      Blocking duration or temperatureHow long, or at what temperature, the membrane was blocked. Either counts. A blocking agent or percentage alone does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the time and temperature together where they sit together. In blocked in 5% milk for 1 h at room temperature mark 1 h at room temperature.
                      Fraction purity shownA statement that subcellular fractions run on the blot were checked for purity or cross-contamination with compartment markers. A fraction marker used only as a loading control, or a fractionation kit named alone, does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the statement. In the purity of nuclear and cytosolic fractions was confirmed with lamin B1 and GAPDH mark the whole phrase. Nothing to mark in nuclear proteins were extracted with the NE-PER kit.
                      Antibody cloneA clone name or number for a monoclonal antibody. A catalog number, or a vendor's epitope code for a polyclonal (Santa Cruz C-20), does not count. Counts for an antibody used in any assay, not only the blot.
                      Mark: Mark every distinct clone id. In anti-beta-actin (clone AC-15, Sigma) mark AC-15.
                      Membrane source or gradeThe supplier, product name or grade of the blot membrane, such as Immobilon-P (Millipore) or Amersham Hybond. Membrane chemistry or pore size alone does not count, and neither does the supplier of a transfer system or kit that does not name the membrane, or an Immobilon ECL substrate, which is the ECL reagent. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the supplier or product words for the membrane. In transferred to PVDF membranes (Immobilon-P, Millipore) mark Immobilon-P, Millipore. Nothing to mark in transferred to PVDF membranes.

                      Ask about a rule

                      This answers questions about what a rule means, nothing else. It is never given the paper, it cannot see what you are reading, and it will refuse if you paste text and ask what the answer is. Deciding what a paper says is the whole job being measured here, so it has to stay yours.

                      Questions are logged with your initials, so ask freely and openly.

                      The rules above are the reference. This is only for when one of them is unclear.
