What counts as a western blot. Western blot means proteins separated on a gel, transferred to a membrane and detected with an antibody. An OxyBlot counts. A dot blot or slot blot, of RNA or of protein, does not; neither does a capillary system such as Wes or Jess, which has no membrane, or a membrane probed only with streptavidin or a lectin, which uses no antibody. Items marked 'Western blot only' do not count the same thing described for another assay in the paper: qPCR, immunofluorescence, IHC, flow cytometry, ELISA, mass spectrometry, or a gel that was stained rather than blotted. Judge the text the page shows, including any figure legend printed in it; a detail found only in a legend or supplement that is not shown is not stated. A method given only by citation ('as previously described [12]') is not stated: mark only what the text itself says. The Shortcut citation item is the exception: it records the citation phrase itself. Some PDFs drop the micro sign or print it as m ('50 g', '25 mg' for micrograms); rule on the intended unit.

Every question carries its own What to mark rule with a worked example. Follow it rather than your instinct: consistency between reviewers matters more here than any single choice. Full rules under Guide.

Results 3.1. Worm EV Isolates Obtained by Differential Ultracentrifugation and Size Exclusion Chromatography Contain Non-EV Contaminants. We first tested two size- based separation methods, differential (ultra)centrifugation (dUC) and size exclusion chromatography (SEC), for isola- tion of EVs released by Schistosoma adult worms. As an indicator for the presence of EVs we used western blot anal- ysis for S. mansoni tetraspanin-2 (TSP-2), which is one of the most abundant proteins identified in adult worm EVs [26, 27]. Both in the EV-enriched 100,000 xg pellet obtained by dUC and in the EV-enriched SEC fractions, we confirmed the presence of TSP-2 containing schistosome EVs (Figures 1(a) and 1(c)). However, we observed brown color- ing of both the UC pellet and the EV-containing SEC frac- tions (Figures 1(b) and 1(d)). We suspected that this was due to contamination with the blood digestion product hemozoin, which is pigmented and colors the worm gut dark and culture medium light brown. We confirmed the pres- ence of hemozoin crystals in 100,000 ×g EV pellets by cryo electron microscopy (cryo-EM) and observed that these crystals overlapped in size with the adult worm EV (Figure 1(e)). This suggests that small hemozoin crystals, and possibly also other non-EV particles [21], sedimented at similar g-forces as the worm EVs and could not be sep- arated from EVs based on size differences. The hemozoin contamination was specific for adult worm EV isolates, as EV-enriched dUC pellets from schistosomula did not contain hemozoin (Figure 1(f)). This was expected because schistosomula are transformed from larvae released by snails and cultures of these worms do not contain blood- digestion products. These data indicate that for the design of a protocol that allows purification of EVs from both schistosome life stages, size-based separation methods do not suffice and should be combined with density gradient centrifugation. 3.2. The Use of Small Density Gradients Increases EV Concentration and Yield. When separation based on size is insufficient, EVs can be further purified using density gradi- ent centrifugation [12, 14, 28]. An unwanted consequence of conventional density gradient separation, however, is the dilution of EVs in relatively large volumes of gradient medium. As a consequence, additional concentrating steps are required for further analysis of the EVs. This can be achieved by ultracentrifugation, after pooling the EV- containing density gradient fractions in a larger UC tube, dilution with PBS, and repelleting by UC [28]. This step can cause loss of EVs via adsorption to tube walls and han- dling time, though the addition of BSA to the PBS may reduce some of the loss [15]. Because loss of EVs is particu- larly undesirable when working with limited worm-derived material, we aimed to optimize density gradient-based EV isolation for low-input material. We hypothesized that using a small volume density gradient in a small tube will reduce EV loss by decreasing the plastic surface area to which EVs may be adsorbed [15]. Iodixanol but Not Sucrose Density Gradients Are Suitable for Purification of Adult Worm and Schistosomula EVs. We aimed to optimize an EV purification protocol suitable for EVs of both adult worms and schistosomula. To verify whether both types of EVs reached equilibrium density within 2 hours of centrifugation in these small gradients, we compared loading the gradients on top of the EV- enriched pellets (EVs floating “bottom-up”) to loading the EVs on top of the gradients (EVs floating “top-down”). TSP-2 detection in collected fractions showed that both schistosomula and adult worm EVs concentrated in frac- tions with densities characteristic for EVs (1.08-1.16 g/ml) and that this was similar in bottom-up or top-down loaded gradients (Figures 3(a) and 3(b)), indicating that equilibrium densities were reached. Another widely used density gradient medium is sucrose, and we additionally tested whether EVs from both schistosome life stages could also be isolated by a small sucrose gradient. However, two hours of centrifugation was not sufficient for the adult worm EVs to reach equi- librium density in bottom-up sucrose gradients (Figure 3(c)). Under these conditions, part of the TSP-2 remained in the fractions with higher densities, suggesting that the EVs needed more time to reach the lower densi- ties. Indeed, 13.5 hours centrifugation of the bottom-up gradient was needed for the adult worm EVs to reach the 1.12-1.16g/ml density fractions (Figure 3(c)). In con- trast to the EVs from the adult worms, sucrose gradients were less effective in purification of schistosomula EVs. In both bottom-up and top-down sucrose gradients, the schistosomula EVs ended up in fractions with higher den- sities (between 1.21 and 1.28 g/ml) compared to iodixanol gradients (Figure 3(d)). This is undesirable, since various non-EV contaminants also remain in the bottom fractions of gradients. These data suggest that the small iodixanol gradients are most optimal and time-efficient for purifying EVs from both life stages of S. mansoni. 3.4. Small Iodixanol Density Gradients Separate EVs from Non-EV Contaminants. Finally, we verified whether the small iodixanol gradients allowed separation of EVs from non-EV contaminants (Figure 1). We selected hemozoin as detectable example for non-EV contaminants, since markers for other protein contaminants are currently lacking for Schistosoma spp. UC pellets of adult worm culture superna- tant were overlaid with the optimized small iodixanol den- sity gradient. After two hours centrifugation, we observed a black substance in the bottom of the gradient, suggesting the presence of hemozoin (Figure 4(a)). Cryo-EM analysis of EV-containing fractions (1.07-1.21 g/ml) confirmed the absence of hemozoin among the high number of adult worm EVs in these gradient fractions (Figure 4(b)). These data indicate that the small iodixanol density gradient allowed successful separation of EVs from non-EV particles such as hemozoin. We propose that small-sized (1.7 ml) iodixanol density gradients can be used to simultaneously separate and concentrate EVs from limited sources, such as hel- minths, to increase EV yield while reducing handling time. 4. Discussion Each EV source has its own challenge for obtaining a high- quality EV preparation with sufficient material for down- stream analysis, and these challenges often include the sepa- ration of EVs from non-EV contaminants [30]. For Schistosoma mansoni parasites, these non-EV contaminants include many released E/S components [17, 18] and for adult worms also the blood-digestion product hemozoin 6 Journal of Immunology Research [31]. Both non-EV E/S components and hemozoin are unwanted in EV-preparations, especially when studying host-pathogen interactions as they can affect host immune responses [32, 33]. Additional challenges for studying EVs from S. mansoni are imposed by the complex life cycle of this parasite. Not only do the different life stages of the par- asite release EVs with different characteristics (Figures 1(e) and 1(f) and [23]), there is also limited availability of each life stage, thus restricting the material to isolate EVs from. This latter restriction makes any loss of EVs during the iso- lation process highly undesirable. Here, we propose the use of small iodixanol density gradients to increase EV yield compared to larger-sized gradients for separating EVs from non-EV contaminants. This protocol is applicable to EVs from the different S. mansoni life stages and EVs from other limited sources. There are several advantages of iodixanol over sucrose gradients for the purification of EVs. Iodixanol is isosmotic and isotonic, which is better to preserve EV struc- ture and function.                       The shortest phrase that states the detail. Not the whole sentence when three words will do, and not the vendor, city or catalog number unless the question asks for them.

                      Every question shows a worked example under What to mark. Follow it rather than your instinct.

                      When a paper gives several

                      Many papers list a table of antibodies with eight RRIDs, eight catalog numbers and several vendors. Mark them all. Press Use highlighted text once per value and they stack up in a numbered list. Press Done, next when the list is complete.

                      If they sit in a contiguous table or a run-on list, selecting the whole block in one go is fine and much faster. You are marking where the evidence is, not counting.

                      The same value repeated does not need marking twice. A paper that uses 1:1000 for six antibodies has one dilution, not six.

                      If you stop before you have them all, tick 'There are more of these than I marked'. That box matters more than it looks: without it, someone who stopped at five of eight cannot be told apart from someone who found everything, and the three they skipped would count against the extractor as mistakes it did not make.

                      What counts

                      Judge only what is written. Not what the authors almost certainly did, and not what is standard practice in the field.

                      Judge the text shown. The page shows the Methods section, and sometimes figure legends or the full text where no Methods heading was found. A detail in the text shown counts, legend included. A detail found only in a legend or supplementary file that is not shown is Not stated.

                      Unclear is a real answer. It is set aside rather than counted either way, so use it when you are genuinely stuck rather than guessing.

                      The shaded sentences

                      The faint tint marks the western blot section: sentences that name the blot, the gel, the membrane or its detection. It is the same for every question on a paper. Blot section jumps between its blocks. Other assays are left untinted, but an item can still be stated elsewhere, for example in a figure legend.

                      The warmer marks are the sentences most likely to hold the answer to the open question, and Prev and Next step between them.

                      Both are search aids and nothing more. Fixed keyword rules pick them; they are never told the answer, they sometimes shade irrelevant sentences and sometimes miss the right one. Read around them, and judge only the text.

                      Every question, in one place

                      Splicing disclosedA statement that lanes were spliced, rearranged or taken from different parts of a blot or from different blots, or that none were. Silence does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the statement. In lanes were rearranged from the same membrane, indicated by dividing lines mark the whole phrase.
                      Transfer conditionsThe voltage or current, the time, or the temperature of the transfer. Any one counts. The transfer method alone ('wet transfer') does not. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the conditions. In transferred at 100 V for 1 h at 4 °C mark the whole phrase.
                      Secondary antibody vendorThe company that supplied a secondary antibody, including the maker of a kit that supplies it. 'All antibodies were from X' counts. Counts for an antibody used in any assay, not only the blot.
                      Mark: Mark every distinct company name. Leave out the city and country. A table can be marked as one block. In HRP-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG (Jackson ImmunoResearch) mark Jackson ImmunoResearch.
                      Substrate incubation timeHow long the membrane was incubated in the detection substrate before imaging. An exposure or imaging time does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the time. In incubated with ECL for 1 min mark 1 min. Nothing to mark in an exposure time.
                      Protease or phosphatase inhibitors namedProtease or phosphatase inhibitors added to the lysis buffer, named as products or compounds, or a cocktail identified by brand name, vendor or catalog number. 'Protease inhibitors' with nothing named or identified does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark each named inhibitor or product. In supplemented with cOmplete and PhosSTOP (Roche) mark cOmplete and PhosSTOP.
                      Transfer buffer compositionThe transfer buffer's recipe (for example Tris-glycine with 20% methanol), or the product used. A recipe with at least one component and its concentration counts, and so does a product identified by brand name, vendor or catalog number. 'Transfer buffer' or 'Tris-glycine buffer' alone does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the recipe, or the product with what identifies it. In in Tris-glycine buffer containing 20% methanol mark the whole phrase.
                      Antibody immunogenThe antigen used to raise an antibody: a peptide sequence or region, a recombinant fragment, or a full-length protein. The target or a phospho-site given as part of the antibody's name (anti-phospho-ERK Thr202/Tyr204) does not count. Counts for an antibody used in any assay, not only the blot.
                      Mark: Mark the words naming the immunogen. In raised against a synthetic peptide corresponding to residues 1-20 of human p53 mark the whole phrase.
                      Exposure timeAn exposure or acquisition time for the blot's signal, on film or on an imager. 'Auto-exposure', 'different exposure times', 'exposed to film' with no time, or a substrate incubation time ('incubated with ECL for 1 min') does not count, and neither does an antibody or cytokine array, which is not a blot. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the time and its unit. In bands were imaged with a 30 s exposure on a ChemiDoc mark 30 s. Nothing to mark in incubated with ECL for 1 min or cells were exposed to 80 uM drug for 30 min.
                      Membrane cutting disclosedA statement that the membrane was cut into strips or pieces before antibody incubation, so that targets were probed on separate pieces. Cropping the image for the figure does not count. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the statement. In membranes were cut at the 50 kDa marker and probed separately mark the whole phrase. Nothing to mark in blots were cropped for clarity.
                      Replicate countA number of replicates stated for the western blot, in the blot's paragraph or a blot figure legend. A group size stated for the blot counts ('six rats from each group' in the Western blot paragraph). A legend-wide count applies to a blot panel only when that panel is quantified. A statistics paragraph that names no assay, or a group size stated elsewhere, does not count. You are not being asked whether they are biological or technical. Western blot only.
                      Mark: Mark the number together with the words that make it a replicate count. In western blots are representative of three independent experiments mark three independent experiments. Nothing to mark in qPCR reactions were run in triplicate or in data are mean ± SD of three independent experiments in a statistics paragraph that names no assay.
